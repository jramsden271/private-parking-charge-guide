# Argument library — left the site

Part of the private-parking-charge-guide argument library (group LS). **Read `arguments.md` first**: its contents list, "How to use" rules, "Gathering evidence" section and IAS note all apply here, and the arguments here often combine with its core groups (L, C, CP, M, CT). Argument IDs are stable across files.

## LS — Left the site

These charges come from a term like "Driver and/or passengers must remain on site while the vehicle is parked", usually at retail parks and shared car parks (UKPC and UKCPS feature often). The operator's evidence is usually photos of the parked car, sometimes a patrol officer's photo of a person walking away. Regulars' view: these are hard for operators to win and rarely reach court (6300836; 5939613, where UKPC reportedly withdrew at POPLA because it couldn't prove the driver left). *Confidence: Medium (forum experience).*

**Playbook:**
- Appeal as keeper (or hirer) as usual: no keeper liability if the NTK fails POFA (L1, L4), plus LS1. For IPC members (e.g. UKCPS) the IAS caution applies.
- **Complain to the retailer or landowner (Plan A)** with any receipt showing a purchase on site during the stay; this often works. Send the receipt to the **retailer, not the operator**: regulars warn it can help identify the driver (5939613). If it's mentioned to the operator at all, describe it about the vehicle ("a purchase was made on site during the vehicle's stay").
- If an attendant photographed people rather than ticketing the car, add CP4 (watched but didn't intervene) and, if the photography was covert, CP5.
- Don't let the user say "nobody left" unless they know it's true. A keeper who doesn't know puts the operator to proof instead.
- **When the user was the driver and genuinely didn't leave, admitting to driving can be the stronger route.** This is one of the SKILL.md section 3 exceptions. The driver can then say, first-hand and truthfully, that they stayed on site (with a receipt from a shop on the site, if they have one), and the operator is put to strict proof that the driver left, which its photos of a parked car can't show. The trade-off: keeper-liability points (L1, L4) are lost for good, so this suits cases where those are weak (e.g. a POFA-compliant NTK) and the driver's own account is strong. Discuss it; the user decides.
  - A middle route: a keeper who knows the facts can say "the driver did not leave the site" without saying who the driver was, keeping the keeper points. But at court, first-hand evidence of that usually means the witness was there, so think ahead to the witness statement.

### LS1. No evidence that anyone left the site, or who they were
- **Point:** the operator must prove the breach. Photos of a parked car show the car was parked, not that anyone left the site. Even a photo of a person walking away doesn't show: that they crossed the site boundary; who they were; that they came from this car; or that they were the driver (as opposed to a passenger, if the term only covers the driver).
- **Applies when:** the evidence is photos of the car only (the usual case: 6300836, and an FTLA thread where the operator had three photos of the car and one of a sign); or a person is photographed but not shown leaving the boundary or clearly identified.
- **Evidence:** the operator's photo pack (check the timestamps: a charge issued minutes after arrival, with no follow-up photos, suggests nobody watched anyone leave); a site plan showing where the boundary is.
- **Strength:** Strong where only the car is photographed; Moderate where a person is photographed walking away (6436773: regulars still advised against conceding anything).
- **Defeated by:** clear photos or CCTV of the identified driver leaving the site boundary; an admission (e.g. "I just popped to the shop next door").
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* "There is no evidence that the driver left the site. The operator is put to strict proof."
  - *POPLA and IAS:* point out exactly what each photo does and doesn't show; ask for any CCTV or patrol notes.
  - *Defence:* deny the breach; put the claimant to strict proof of who left, when, and where the boundary was.
  - *WS:* comment on each photo; site plan as an exhibit.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 6300836; 5939613; 6436773; 6677710 (UKPC/DCB Legal, four PCNs, "driver left site designated for customer parking only": photos of the parked car only and a "may be issued" sign; regulars expected discontinuance; DCB dropped it at mediation, Nov 2026).

### LS2. A passenger isn't a party to the contract
- **Point:** the parking contract is made with the driver, by conduct (entering and parking). Passengers do nothing that accepts an offer, so no contract is formed with them, and a term purporting to bind "passengers" can't bind them. The operator then has to argue that the driver accepted a term making them answerable for other people's conduct. Such a term is unusual, unclear and arguably unfair (Consumer Rights Act 2015 s.62; and s.68 transparency), and where it's ambiguous the meaning most favourable to the consumer prevails (s.69).
- **Applies when:** the term mentions passengers, and the evidence (if any) shows a passenger rather than the driver; or it's unclear who left.
- **Strength:** Moderate as a supporting point; not tested in a reported case that we know of. *Confidence: Low–Medium.* Lead with LS1; use this second.
- **By stage:**
  - *POPLA and IAS:* a short paragraph after LS1.
  - *Defence:* plead that no contract was formed with any passenger, and that a term making the driver liable for others' conduct is unfair and not transparent.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet; search "passengers must remain on site")*

### LS3. "The site" isn't defined, or the boundary isn't clear
- **Point:** a term requiring people to stay "on site" is only clear if the site is defined. At retail parks, shared car parks and mixed sites, shops, cafés and the car park may share a boundary or overlap, and there's often no marking. A customer using a shop or café next to or within the retail park may not have "left the site" at all. The Code requires sign terms to be "clear, unambiguous" (Single Code v1.1 cl. 3.1.3(i)) and, on shared-use land, to "indicate clearly … whether and where different terms and conditions apply" (cl. 3.1.3(l)).
- **Applies when:** the signs don't define the site or show a map; the place visited was arguably part of the same development; boundaries are unmarked.
- **Evidence:** photos or Street View of the layout, the signs and any map on them (see "Gathering evidence" in `arguments.md`); the landowner's site plan (put to proof under L5).
- **Strength:** Moderate to strong at retail parks; a forum user won a case where "the site" was unspecified (5038493).
- **By stage:** *POPLA and IAS:* describe the layout with photos. *Defence:* put the claimant to proof of the boundary of "the site" and where it's shown to drivers. *WS:* exhibits.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 5038493.

### LS4. The term is vague or conditional
- **Point:** signs often say people who leave "may be liable to" a charge. A term that only says a charge *may* apply isn't a clear promise to pay a stated sum. Unclear terms are construed against the trader (CRA 2015 s.69).
- **Applies when:** the sign wording is conditional ("may", "could") or doesn't clearly link leaving the site to a specific charge.
- **Strength:** Supporting.
- **By stage:** *POPLA, IAS and defence:* quote the sign's exact words.
- **Forum threads:** 6300836.

### LS5. No legitimate interest in penalising a short customer visit
- **Point:** *Beavis* rested on the operator's interest in turnover of spaces for the retailers' customers. Where the car stayed within the free or paid time and its occupants were customers of the park, charging for walking to a neighbouring unit serves no such interest; the charge is a penalty.
- **Applies when:** the stay was within the permitted time, and there's evidence of spending on site (keep the receipt for the retailer complaint and the WS, described about the vehicle).
- **Strength:** Supporting.
- **By stage:** *Defence:* brief. *WS:* expand.
- **Forum threads:** 5939613.
