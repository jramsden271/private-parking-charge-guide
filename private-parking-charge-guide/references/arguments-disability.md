# Argument library — disability

Part of the private-parking-charge-guide argument library (group DS). **Read `arguments.md` first**: its contents list, "How to use" rules, "Gathering evidence" section and IAS note all apply here, and the arguments here often combine with its core groups (L, C, CP, M, CT). Argument IDs are stable across files.

## DS — Disability (driver or passenger)

Use when the driver or a passenger is disabled, visibly or not, whether or not they hold a Blue Badge. The Single Code's section 4 ("Accessible parking") is unusually helpful here. Its commentary says operators "need to have regard to the obligations placed upon them by the Equality Act 2010 and the EHRC statutory code, in particular to make reasonable adaptations to accommodate disabled people", that "adaptations are not purely physical - people with other disabilities might reasonably need longer consideration period and grace periods, more time to access payment machinery, and other ways to pay where payment is required", and that "Recognition of these obligations is important in the consideration of appeals."

**Before using any DS point:**
- **Who was disabled, and does saying so identify the driver?** A disabled *passenger* can often be mentioned without identifying the driver ("a disabled passenger was in the vehicle"), but not always: if the household is a couple and one is disabled and can't drive, naming the passenger may effectively name the driver. Think it through with the user.
- **Does it clash with another point?** In a left-the-site charge (LS), explaining where a disabled passenger went admits someone left. Keep the appeal on LS1/LS3 (no evidence, unclear site) and put the disability and the reason for the trip in the **landowner complaint**, not the appeal. If the disability is the *driver's*, using it usually means naming them: that's the SKILL.md section 3 exception, so discuss it with the user and let them decide.
- **Share only what's needed.** Health details are sensitive. Describe the effect ("needs extra time to get in and out of the car and to reach the machine") rather than a diagnosis where possible. Send a Blue Badge copy with the personal details and photo redacted except what proves validity (the forum redacts; 6679995).
- **Complaint first.** Disability cases often do best as a complaint to the landowner or retailer (Plan A) framed as a reasonable-adjustments request, alongside the appeal. If the retailer is only a **tenant** (as Screwfix said it was in 6679995), ask it to escalate to its estates or property team, and ask the operator who its client actually is (L5). Even a retailer's request can be refused by the operator; in that thread UKPC refused Screwfix's request and later said it had reduced the charge to £45 "following our client's instruction".
- **Paying a reduced sum is a legitimate choice.** In 6679995 the keeper paid UKPC's £20 offer to end it rather than go to POPLA; regulars were split between principle and pragmatism. Lay out both and let the user decide (SKILL.md section 7). A complaint to the CMA (or the trade body) can still be made after paying.
- **Honesty:** never imply a Blue Badge holder or disabled person was present if they weren't.

### DS1. Reasonable adjustments (Equality Act 2010)
- **Point:** a parking operator providing a service must make reasonable adjustments for disabled people (Equality Act 2010, ss. 20–21 and 29), and must not treat someone unfavourably because of something arising from their disability (s. 15). Where the "breach" was caused by the disability (extra time needed to get out of the car, to reach or use the machine or app, to read the signs; an anxiety or cognitive condition affecting payment or registration; a medical episode), insisting on the full charge can be a failure to adjust. The Code's own commentary agrees (above), including longer consideration and grace periods and other ways to pay.
- **Applies when:** the disability plausibly explains the overstay, non-payment or registration error; visible or hidden disabilities; Blue Badge or not.
- **Evidence:** a short statement of the effect of the disability on what happened; a Blue Badge copy (redacted) or other proof if the user is willing; times showing the extra time needed.
- **Strength:** Moderate at appeal and POPLA/IAS; strong as a complaint to the landowner or retailer (who also owes duties under the Act). In court it's a defence point, not a separate claim, unless the user wants advice on a discrimination claim (recommend specialist advice). *Confidence: Medium.*
- **Defeated by:** no link between the disability and the alleged breach.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* the effect of the disability, the adjustment that should have been made, and a request to cancel.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* add the Code's cl. 4 commentary and the operator's failure to consider it ("Recognition of these obligations is important in the consideration of appeals").
  - *Complaint:* to the landowner or retailer, as a request for a reasonable adjustment.
  - *Defence and WS:* plead the duty and the failure; evidence in the WS.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 6679995 (Coupon-mad suggested framing a refused landowner cancellation as a reasonable-adjustment issue in a CMA report); 6597735 (dropping a disabled person at a Blue Badge assessment).
- **Sources:** Equality Act 2010 ss. 15, 20, 21, 29; Single Code v1.1 section 4 commentary.

### DS2. Blue Badge held but not displayed
- **Point:** where a valid Blue Badge holder was in the vehicle (as driver or passenger) but the badge wasn't displayed, the Code says the charge should be reduced to £20 at most (Annex F.3(e): the holder "supplies a copy of the Blue Badge they hold, that would have been valid at the time… i.e. that the holder was in the vehicle whether or not as the driver"; any tariff is still payable). That's a minimum concession, not a ceiling on arguments: many operators cancel, and a landowner complaint often gets it cancelled. Also check the signs: where the scheme is recognised and display is required, cl. 4.2 says signs must make clear where the badge is to be displayed and what the driver must do.
- **Careful:** a Blue Badge has no automatic legal force on private land; it matters because the site's terms (or the operator's Equality Act duties) recognise it. Whether a badge-holder passenger entitled the car to use a disabled bay depends on the signs and terms (*Medium*).
- **Applies when:** a valid badge holder was in the vehicle; disabled bay or general bay.
- **Disabled-bay charges, extra checks (6679995):**
  - **Markings aren't terms.** As with any road marking on private land (see NS3), a wheelchair symbol or yellow box painted on the ground carries no contractual weight by itself. The term (that the bay is reserved for Blue Badge holders, what must be displayed, and the charge for breaching it) has to be on a sign that the driver could see and read, and the usual consideration period and signage rules apply (C1, C6; Code cl. 3.1.3, 4.1, 4.2). Put the operator to proof of the sign at or near the bay and its wording, not just the markings. In 6679995 the operator's photos showed no visible bay markings or signs at all.
  - **Read the exact terms:** what do they require? Display only? That the holder stays with the car, or is the driver? Don't accept the operator's gloss; James_Poisson asked where the contract said the holder had to stay in the car.
  - **"Instant contravention" isn't right:** the consideration period still applies to disabled bays; the Code's own example of time needed is "a driver seeking a Blue Badge parking bay" (cl. 5.1(a) note). A charge built from a 20-second photo sequence gives no time to realise the bay is reserved or to put the badge out (C6, CP4).
- **Evidence:** a redacted badge copy (valid on the date); the sign wording about Blue Badges; the operator's photos of the bay (markings, sign).
- **Strength:** Moderate: the Code floor is £20 (6679995: UKPC reduced to £20 "as a gesture of goodwill" after seeing the badge; the keeper paid rather than go to POPLA). Combine with DS1 and a landowner complaint to aim for cancellation.
- **By stage:** *Appeal:* badge copy plus DS1. *POPLA and IAS:* F.3(e) and cl. 4.2. *Defence:* supporting.
- **Forum threads:** 6679995.
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 Annex F.3(e), cl. 4.2 (`notices-and-codes.md`).

### DS3. Accessibility rules on signs
- **Point:** cl. 4.1 requires "at least one sign containing the terms and conditions for parking" that "can be viewed without the driver needing to leave the vehicle". Cl. 4.2 requires clear signs about any Blue Badge requirements, and separate, prominent, low-placed signs (with repeaters) where Blue Badge holders can't use yellow lines on the site. Front-line staff must have disability awareness training (cl. 13.1.2).
- **Applies when:** the only terms sign is high up, far from the bay, or not readable from a car (6679995: a poster argued the sign was too high to read from a car); Blue Badge terms or yellow-line rules weren't signed.
- **Strength:** Supporting; Moderate at POPLA/IAS with photos.
- **Forum threads:** 6679995.
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 4.1, 4.2, 13.1.2.
