# Argument library — site types with their own rules

Part of the private-parking-charge-guide argument library (group ST). **Read `arguments.md` first**: its contents list, "How to use" rules, "Gathering evidence" section and IAS note all apply here, and the arguments here combine with its core groups (L, C, CP, M, CT). Argument IDs are stable across files. Airports, stations and residential sites have their own files (`arguments-airports.md`, `arguments-no-stopping.md`, `arguments-residential.md`).

## ST — Site types

Some kinds of site come with extra rules, regulators or people to complain to. The core points (keeper liability, signage, grace and consideration periods) still lead; these entries add what's specific to the site.

### ST1. Hospitals (England)
- **Point:** NHS trusts in England must follow the government's NHS car parking guidance (2022), which is mandatory through the NHS Standard Contract. Parking must be free for those "in greatest need": disabled people (Blue Badge holders, as patients, visitors or staff), frequent outpatient attenders (at least 3 appointments within a month, over at least 3 months), parents of sick children staying overnight, and staff working night shifts. The guidance also makes trusts responsible for the actions of private parking contractors. So a charge to someone in one of these groups is a strong complaint to the trust, and a charge arising from a delayed appointment or an emergency is strong mitigation.
- **Playbook:**
  - **Complain to the trust** (Plan A): patients and visitors through PALS; staff through the trust's estates or parking office, and a union rep. Ask the trust to cancel; it is responsible for its contractor. SKILL.md section 5A has the routes.
  - **Appeal as keeper** as usual. Ordinary hospital car parks are normally relevant land (*Medium*), so the keeper points depend on the NTK (L1).
  - **Evidence:** appointment letters or texts showing the time and any delay (keep it about the vehicle where possible); proof of the concession (Blue Badge, outpatient schedule, staff rota, a child's admission).
  - **Disability:** see `arguments-disability.md`.
- **Strength:** Strong as a complaint where the user is in a free-parking group or the overstay was caused by the hospital; moderate as an appeal point.
- **Careful:** this guidance covers England. Hospital parking is generally free in Wales and Scotland (out of scope here anyway). Some trusts run parking in-house; the complaint is still to the trust.
- **Forum threads:** 6661706 (staff EV bay at a hospital).
- **Sources:** DHSC, NHS car parking guidance 2022 (updated 29 March 2022): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/nhs-patient-visitor-and-staff-car-parking-principles

### ST2. Motorway and trunk-road service areas
- **Point:** under DfT Circular 01/2022 (England), official service areas (those signed from the motorway or trunk road) must provide at least 2 hours' free parking for all vehicle types, as a condition of their road signs. Most operators charge after 2 or 3 hours. So: a sign giving less than 2 hours free at an official service area breaches the Circular (complain to the operator and National Highways), and an overstay is still an overstay once the free period is used, so the ordinary points apply (grace and consideration periods CP1/C6, signage C7/C8, keeper liability).
- **Signs at services:** the Code's Annex A sets the largest entrance-sign requirements for service areas on motorways and dual carriageways, because drivers arrive at speed (C7). Hotel guests usually have to register their registration at reception for free parking: a failure to register is a PY5-type point.
- **Strength:** Moderate; overstays well past the free period are hard to win on site-specific points alone, so lead with keeper liability and signage.
- **Careful:** a service area's free parking can apply to some car parks and not others; read the signs for the area used.
- **Sources:** DfT Circular 01/2022 (strategic road network and the delivery of sustainable development), on roadside facilities; Single Code v1.1 Annex A.

### ST3. Petrol stations and forecourts
- **Point:** a car queuing for a pump, using the pump, or waiting for or using a car wash isn't "parking" for the time it was held up, and a queue the driver couldn't avoid is a compelled wait, not a choice. Charges for this are under official scrutiny: on 15 July 2026 the CMA opened a consumer-law investigation into Euro Car Parks that will consider "whether it's fair for some drivers to receive parking charges while queuing for, or using, petrol pumps" and car washes (information-gathering runs to Spring 2027; no finding yet). Combine with C6 (consideration period not a cut-off; time needed for queues and traffic within the site, cl. 5.1(f)–(g)) and C4 (stopping isn't parking).
- **Applies when:** the "overstay" is explained by a queue for fuel, the shop, a car wash, air or vacuum; or the site is busy.
- **Evidence:** fuel or car wash receipt with the time; the ANPR times; photos or a description of the queue; bank records.
- **Playbook:** complain to the forecourt operator or retailer with the receipt (Plan A); appeal as keeper; mention the CMA investigation as context (it isn't a ruling). A complaint to the CMA with evidence is also possible.
- **Strength:** Moderate to strong with a receipt showing the visit was for fuel or a wash; *Confidence: Medium* (the CMA investigation is ongoing; check for updates).
- **Sources:** CMA case page, Euro Car Parks consumer protection enforcement case: https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/euro-car-parks-consumer-protection-enforcement-case ; CMA open letter, 16 July 2026.

### ST4. Fast-food and other "customers only" car parks
- **Point:** restaurants (McDonald's is the classic) often have a short maximum stay (commonly 90 minutes), "customers only while on the premises", and sometimes "no return within 60 minutes" or "drivers and passengers must remain on the premises". POPLA tends to uphold clear overstays (5721721), so the best route is usually the **complaint to the restaurant** with proof the driver was a customer: franchisees and head offices often get charges cancelled for genuine customers, especially where slow service caused the overstay. Escalate to head office if the local manager won't help, and get any promise in writing.
- **Applies when:** a genuine customer overstayed (slow service, a party, a queue); the terms were changed recently (C12, Code cl. 3.4); there was no sign inside the restaurant about the time limit; a "left the site" allegation (see `arguments-left-site.md`).
- **Evidence:** the receipt, bank or app order history (time-stamped); photos of the signs; anything showing the wait.
- **Careful:** regulars point out that the consideration period can't simply be added once you've parked, though the grace period applies (FTLA; see CP1 for the CMA answer). Don't claim "the operator can't charge a customer": the terms usually apply to customers too.
- **Strength:** Strong as a complaint with a receipt; moderate as an appeal.
- **Forum threads:** 6554609; 6123579 (complaint to McDonald's CEO before POPLA); 6225369 (manager refused to act); 5721721 (POPLA lost on a 19-minute overstay).

### ST5. Electric vehicle charging bays
- **Point:** charges for "EV bay misuse" (not charging, or staying after charging ended) or for overstaying while charging. Points that work:
  - **No contractual sign at the bay.** Often the only signs at the chargers are the charge provider's instructions, not the parking operator's terms. Coupon-mad: appeal on the basis there are no contractual signs at the EV bays, complain to the landowner, and copy the charge provider, which may lease that bit of land (6596296). Compare C11.
  - **The bay isn't clearly marked as a charging bay, or the sign says "EV parking" rather than "EV charging only".** POPLA allowed an appeal where the EV bay was indistinguishable from others and the sign used the banned words "Penalty Charge Notice" (6628689; C12). Bays with no working charger connected also undermine "misuse" (6661706).
  - **The charger failed.** Evidence of attempts to charge (app logs, photos of the cable connected) shows the driver tried to use the bay as intended (6565890, 6552469).
  - **Overstay while charging.** The charging invoice shows the time spent charging; argue the consideration and grace periods and any confusion between charging time and parking time on the signs (6629171). Weaker if the sign clearly sets a maximum stay for chargers.
- **Careful:** an EV charging network's own "idle" or overstay fee is a separate thing from a private parking charge; check which it is. A Blue Badge doesn't override charging-bay terms by itself.
- **Strength:** Moderate to strong where signs at the bay are missing or unclear; weak where a non-charging vehicle sat in a clearly signed charging-only bay.
- **Forum threads:** 6596296; 6628689; 6661706; 6565890; 6552469; 6629171; 6575874; 6652963.
