# Who's who — operators, debt collectors and solicitors

Reference file for the private-parking-charge-guide skill.


Use these tables to identify documents and to set expectations. They describe **forum-reported tendencies, not guarantees** — firms change their paperwork, solicitors and litigation habits. Always read the actual NTK against POFA Sch 4 rather than relying on the operator's reputation. Last checked 2026-11-12. If you have web access, search the forum for recent threads on the same operator + solicitor before predicting what will happen.

## Parking operators

**Trade body:** operators sometimes switch between the BPA (→ POPLA) and the IPC (→ IAS), so read it from the notice or rejection letter rather than from this table.

**POFA column:** the default is **mixed / unknown — check the actual NTK** against POFA Sch 4 every time. Only a few operators have a settled forum reputation, and those are flagged. A reputation is a reason to look closely, never a substitute for reading the notice.

| Operator (aliases) | NTK / POFA reputation | Litigation pattern (forum experience) |
|---|---|---|
| **NCP (National Car Parks)** | **Reputation: often non-POFA wording** (listed in the newbies thread) → a no-keeper-liability argument is often available. | **Entered administration on 16 March 2026** (PwC administrators); the business, brand and most car parks were **sold on 8 October 2026 to NCP UK Trade Co Ltd** (Martin Property Group); some sites closed or moved to other operators. Claims go via **Moorside Legal**. Forum defences add a short paragraph: a company in administration can't commence or continue litigation without the administrators' express permission, so the claimant is put to strict proof that the administrators authorised the claim and instructed Moorside. Also check **which entity is the claimant** (National Car Parks Ltd in administration vs the new owner) and the date of the parking event relative to the sale. Forum expects these claims to be discontinued, but the user must still defend on time. Don't cc NCP itself on court correspondence. *Confidence: Medium-high (administration and sale dates, from news reports as of Nov 2026) / Medium (litigation effect).* |
| **APCOA** | **Reputation: often non-POFA wording** (newbies thread). Runs many railway station car parks — railway land became "relevant land" on 26 Dec 2025, so check the incident date (relevant land in `notices-and-codes.md`). | — |

## Debt collectors (stages C and C2 — all ignorable)

None of these is a bailiff (except DCBL after a CCJ — see SKILL.md section 4), and none can add anything to a credit file. Letters usually demand ~£160–£170 and threaten "further action". *Confidence: Medium-high.*

| Name | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Debt Recovery Plus (DRP)** | Very common. Also writes as **Zenith Collections** and **Parking Collection Services (PCS)** — same stable. Zenith letters usually mean they're about to give up, sometimes with a "reduced offer". |
| **ZZPS** | Common; may also send letters on **QDR Solicitors** letterhead — a typical "fake LBC" (SKILL.md section 4): check for the 30-day deadline. |
| **Trace Debt Recovery (TRACE)** | Common, including for airport drop-off charges and UKCPS. |
| **DCBL (Direct Collection Bailiffs Ltd)** | Sister company of DCB Legal. "Notice of Debt Recovery" = ignore. Check there is no CCJ. |

If you can't tell whether a letter is from a debt collector or a solicitor's genuine Letter of Claim, apply the 30-day test in SKILL.md section 4; if still unsure, treat it as a possible LBC and ask the user for the full letter.

## Solicitors / litigators (stages D–G)

| Firm | Typical clients | Forum-reported pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **DCB Legal** | ParkingEye, ECP, UKPC, Group Nexus/CP Plus/Highview, Smart, Britannia, I Park, CEL, Excel/VCS | Bulk claims; most go to default judgment because people don't defend. **Defended single-PCN claims are almost always discontinued**, usually just before the claimant's hearing/trial fee is due (about 4 weeks before the hearing, roughly 2 weeks before witness statements are due). The forum has a running thread of hundreds of discontinuances: https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6377263/dcb-legal-record-of-private-parking-court-claim-discontinuations. Exceptions: **multiple PCNs** (more money at stake) and **Excel/VCS** claims are more likely to reach a hearing. ParkingEye claims via DCB Legal often fail CPR 16.4 (→ Chan/Akande strike-out). *Confidence: Medium-high.* |
| **Moorside Legal** | PCM, UKCPS, NCP (in administration — see Parking operators) | Usually discontinues, often late (sometimes the day before the hearing); sometimes simply doesn't turn up. Defendants have recovered costs. Doesn't always discontinue — prepare properly. *Confidence: Medium.* |
| **BW Legal** | Various (historically Excel, PPM, CPM) | Always files a "reply to defence" (standard, meant to intimidate — not a reason to worry). Does take some cases to hearing, often using third-party advocates. *Confidence: Medium.* |
| **Gladstones** | IPC-member operators historically (e.g. UKCPM) | Historically issued large numbers of claims and does take some to hearing. *Confidence: Medium (older data).* |
| **ELMS Legal** | Excel | Historically filed claims for Excel, which then took them back in-house. Recently less seen. |
| **ParkingEye in-house legal team** | ParkingEye | More likely to proceed than DCB Legal claims; prepare properly. |

**Discontinuance timing tip:** if a hearing is listed, the date the claimant must pay its hearing fee is on the hearing notice — usually about 4 weeks before the hearing, roughly 2 weeks before witness statements are due. Users can check with the court after the fee date whether the claim is still live — but must never miss their own deadlines on the assumption the claimant will discontinue.

**Late discontinuance:** the later the discontinuance (especially after witness statements are filed), the better the defendant's chance of recovering limited costs. Recommend the forum.
