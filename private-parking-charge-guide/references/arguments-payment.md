# Argument library — payment problems

Part of the private-parking-charge-guide argument library (group PY). **Read `arguments.md` first**: its contents list, "How to use" rules, "Gathering evidence" section and IAS note all apply here, and the arguments here often combine with its core groups (L, C, CP, M, CT). Argument IDs are stable across files.

## PY — Payment problems

Use when the user paid (or tried to pay) and still got a charge, or when the operator's own systems got the visit wrong. These are among the most common threads on the forum.

**Playbook:**
- **Keeper appeal first, as usual.** Lead with no keeper liability where the NTK allows it (L1, L2, L4); then the payment point. Regulars often say a payment story alone "can't win at POPLA" (e.g. a wrong location code), so it should support, not replace, the keeper points (6081414, 6596906).
- **Complain to the retailer or landowner too (Plan A)** with the payment evidence: a customer who paid, or tried to, is exactly who a retailer wants the charge cancelled for. Often the quickest route.
- **Get the evidence together early:** payment receipts or app screenshots (showing date, time, location code, registration); bank or card statements; photos or video of a broken machine and its screen; proof of no phone signal (screenshots, witnesses); phone location history (e.g. Google Maps timeline) for visits.
- **Payment evidence can identify the driver.** An app account or card in someone's name points to them. Describe it about the vehicle where possible ("payment was made for the vehicle at 10:52") and think before sending account screenshots with names (SKILL.md section 3).
- **The Code's Appeals Charter often helps** (`notices-and-codes.md`): F.1(h) minor keying error → cancel; F.1(j) paid in full before leaving an ANPR car park → cancel; F.3(a) major keying error → £20; F.3(f) machines out of order with no alternative → £20; F.3(g) failed to enter the registration into a terminal → £20. These are the minimum the operator must do, not a limit on the user's arguments.

### PY1. "Double dip": two visits recorded as one long stay
- **Point:** ANPR cameras sometimes miss the first exit and the second entry, so two short visits look like one long stay (e.g. a morning and an evening visit become "10 hours"). The Code requires a manual quality-control check of ANPR images before a charge is issued (cl. 7.3(d)), and its Note 1 names "double dipping" as exactly what that check is for. A charge issued on a double dip breaches the Code, and the operator obtained the keeper's DVLA data without reasonable cause.
- **Applies when:** the user (or keeper) knows the car visited twice; the "stay" spans times the car was elsewhere (overnight, a work day).
- **Evidence:** proof the car was elsewhere in between: receipts, bank or card records, phone location history, work records, other car park tickets. Ask the operator for **all** ANPR images of the vehicle that day, including unmatched ("orphan") images. They often won't produce them, which helps.
- **Defeated by:** a site term limiting visits (e.g. "no return within 2 hours" or "one visit per day"); regulars say such terms are fairly rare, so check the signs (6629177). Even then, the operator has to show the **same driver** returned, which ANPR can't do (C13 in `arguments.md`), so explain the two visits about the vehicle, without saying who drove.
- **Strength:** Strong with evidence of the second visit or of the car being elsewhere. Operators often cancel at appeal.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* "This is an ANPR 'double dip' error: the vehicle made two separate visits. Your Code requires manual checks of ANPR data to detect exactly this (cl. 7.3(d) and Note 1). Cancel the charge." Add no keeper liability where it applies.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* the evidence of two visits; the Code clause; the request for all images.
  - *Defence and WS:* plead two separate visits and the Code failure; exhibit the evidence.
- **Established wording:** *(appeal, from forum threads)* "This is a clear case of the well-known ANPR 'double-dip' error. The system has incorrectly recorded a single continuous stay. You are required to manually check ANPR data, including orphan images, which will confirm the two separate visits." Optionally: "As a result, the keeper's data has been unlawfully obtained from the DVLA." (6595235, 6639580)
- **Forum threads:** 5550336 (double-dip cases list); 6328330; 6350855; 6595235; 6639580 (APCOA station double dip, 2025); 6642807; 6629177.
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 7.3(d) and Note 1.

### PY2. The machine was out of order, and there was no workable way to pay
- **Point:** the operator must show near each payment machine what happens if it's unavailable: whether there are alternative ways to pay, where other machines are, and "whether inability to pay due to machine failure absolves the driver from making payment or whether inability to pay means the driver is not permitted to park and is required to leave within the appropriate consideration period" (Code cl. 6.1.2(c)). It "should provide at least one alternative means of payment" (Note 4). If the machine was broken and there was no workable alternative (no signal for the app or phone line, no other machine), the driver couldn't perform the payment term. Regulars also argue frustration of contract (6273320). The Code's own floor: reduce to £20 where machines were out of operation, there was no alternative, and other working machines weren't reasonably accessible (F.3(f)).
- **Applies when:** the machine was out of order or not accepting payment; the app, phone line or card reader failed; there was no signal; the sign didn't say what to do if the machine failed.
- **Evidence:** photos or video of the machine's screen with the time; screenshots showing no signal or a failed app payment; witnesses (other drivers); the sign wording near the machine (does it say what to do?); payment attempts on bank records.
- **Defeated by:** a working alternative that the driver could reasonably have used (a second machine nearby, a working app with signal), especially if the sign said so (the operator will quote it: 5600292); a later payment that was never made when it could have been.
- **Strength:** Moderate with good evidence; strong where the sign said nothing about machine failure and there was no signal. POPLA has rejected some of these (6227245), so keep the keeper points in front.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* the facts and evidence, plus cl. 6.1.2(c) and F.3(f).
  - *POPLA and IAS:* Code clauses with photos; put the operator to proof that a working alternative existed and was signed at the machine.
  - *Defence and WS:* impossibility (frustration), Code breach, and the evidence.
- **Established wording:** *(POPLA, approved by Coupon-mad, "Nicely done", 6620986)* "Payment Machine Failure Made Compliance Impossible … The driver correctly attempted to pay using the on-site machine. However, at the relevant time, the machine displayed: 'NOT IN USE' … [The operator] does not establish the alternate methods were available and usable."
- **Also:** if the driver left because they couldn't pay, that's a consideration-period point too (C6): they declined the contract and left.
- **Forum threads:** 6620986; 6273320; 6329833; 6383441; 6036715; 6227245 (POPLA rejected).
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 6.1.2(c) and Note 4; Annex F.3(f).

### PY3. Paid, but the payment wasn't matched: wrong location code, wrong app, wrong car, system error
- **Point:** the user paid, but the operator's system didn't link it to this car at this car park. Common causes: a one-digit error in the app's location code; an app that auto-selected a nearby car park; paying through a different app (e.g. RingGo where the site uses PayByPhone); selecting another car in the app; a website or app glitch that didn't take the money. The operator has lost nothing it was entitled to (the tariff was paid, just misdirected), so a £100 charge looks penal, and a retailer or landowner usually wants paying customers left alone.
- **How the Code helps:** a registration mistyped with look-alike characters is a minor keying error → cancel (F.1(h)); other registration errors → £20 (F.3(a)) (see CP2). A wrong **location** code isn't expressly covered by the Charter; argue it as the same kind of honest keying error, and ask the operator to apply the spirit of F.1(h)/F.3(a).
- **Applies when:** there's proof of payment for the right date and time, close to the right place or car.
- **Evidence:** the receipt or app screenshot (date, time, amount, location code, registration); a photo of the car park sign showing the correct code; for a wrong-car payment, evidence both cars are yours (V5C) if needed; a refund request for the wrong session (shows good faith).
- **Defeated by:** evidence too thin to tie the payment to the visit (POPLA rejected a screenshot that didn't show which app was used, 6122593); paying at a different location far away.
- **Strength:** Weak to moderate alone at POPLA (regulars: these circumstances "can't win at POPLA" on their own, 6081414); stronger as a complaint to the landowner or retailer, and as support for the keeper points. Operators often offer £20.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* proof of payment and the honest error; ask for cancellation (or the Charter minimum).
  - *Complaint:* to the retailer or landowner with the proof of payment.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* lead with the keeper points; payment as support.
  - *Defence and WS:* no loss; the charge is penal for an honest error with payment made (C3).
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 6081414; 5900210 (paid via the wrong app); 6122593 (POPLA: evidence insufficient); 6572400 (website error, payment not taken); 6654237, 6657832, 6610003 (wrong location code); 6394257 (wrong registration in JustPark).
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 Annex F.1(h), F.3(a) (`notices-and-codes.md`).

### PY4. Paid late, or paid before leaving
- **Point:** at an ANPR car park, a charge "must not be pursued" where "payment has been made for the full period of parking prior to the vehicle leaving the car park" (Code F.1(j)): this covers the timing of payment only, not underpayment. So a driver who paid late but before leaving is covered. Where payment failed at first (e.g. no signal) and was made as soon as the driver realised, the operator has its tariff; regulars suggest making the payment and telling the operator, with evidence, rather than a formal appeal (6678738). For drop-off zones, see `arguments-airports.md` (a late drop-off payment is not a keying error, and the keeper points lead).
- **Applies when:** payment for the full stay was made before the car left (F.1(j)); or payment was made late for a reason outside the driver's control.
- **Evidence:** payment receipt with time; ANPR exit time (from the NTK); evidence of the failed first attempt (bank record, app error, no signal).
- **Defeated by:** payment after the car left (F.1(j) doesn't apply; it's then mitigation, M1); underpayment.
- **Strength:** Strong where F.1(j) applies; moderate otherwise.
- **By stage:** *Appeal:* cite F.1(j) with the receipt. *POPLA and IAS:* the operator's failure to cancel under F.1(j) is a Code breach (CP3).
- **Forum threads:** 6678738; 6584436.
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 Annex F.1(j).

### PY5. A customer who didn't register the car on the in-store tablet or terminal
- **Point:** many free customer car parks (gyms, restaurants, pubs, shops) require the registration to be entered on a tablet or terminal inside. Where the vehicle "would have been permitted to park at the location, but the driver failed to enter their registration into a terminal/device as specified", the Code's floor is £20 (F.3(g)). The Code also says that where terms require something to be done off site, such as entering the registration "inside a pub or restaurant, the sign should make clear how this is to be done" (cl. 3.1 Note 4). These are often cancelled outright after a complaint to the business with a receipt.
- **Applies when:** the driver was a genuine customer; the requirement to register wasn't clear on the signs, or the terminal wasn't obvious or wasn't working.
- **Evidence:** the receipt (keep it about the vehicle, and send it to the business rather than the operator if it might identify the driver); photos of the signs and the terminal.
- **Strength:** Strong as a complaint to the business; moderate at appeal (operators often offer £20).
- **By stage:** *Complaint:* to the business, with the receipt. *Appeal:* the signs didn't make the requirement clear (cl. 3.1 Note 4); at least F.3(g). *POPLA, IAS, defence:* signage (C1, C8) and the keeper points.
- **Forum threads:** 5761546 (Smart Parking: faulty registration machine, cancelled after a receipt).
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 3.1 Note 4; Annex F.3(g).
