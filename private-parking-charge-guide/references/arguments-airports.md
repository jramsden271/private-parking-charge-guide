# Argument library — airport charges

Part of the private-parking-charge-guide argument library (group AP). **Read `arguments.md` first**: its contents list, "How to use" rules, "Gathering evidence" section and IAS note all apply here, and the arguments here often combine with its core groups (L, C, CP, M, CT). ## AP — Airport charges (drop-off zones, no-stopping roads, car parks)

Main forum source: Coupon-mad's group thread **6500170** ("Heathrow Gatwick Manchester & other Airport drop-off PCNs - easy: how to appeal & win"; opened Jan 2024, edited Aug 2025, still active). Its headline advice is not to pay drop-off PCNs. Check the latest posts before advising, since the thread says the advice will change when the government's statutory regime arrives (don't predict that regime; see SKILL.md section 9).

**For a keeper, at any airport, lead with no keeper liability** (L3 byelaw land, L4, and for a drop-off or roadside stop NS10 in `arguments-no-stopping.md`), whichever kind of charge it is; then add the kind-specific points. Check the airport's byelaws cover stopping or parking (6654021).

**Next, work out which kind of airport charge it is.**
| Kind | Typical operators and airports (per 6500170) | Trade body | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid drop-off zone**: the fee wasn't paid by the deadline | APCOA (Heathrow, Manchester, Luton, Birmingham); NCP (Gatwick); NSL (Stansted) | BPA, so POPLA | AP1, AP2, L3, L4 |
| **No-stopping roadways** | VCS (Bristol, East Midlands, Leeds Bradford, Southend); UKPPO (Newcastle) | IPC, so IAS | L3, L4, NS10, then the NS group in `arguments-no-stopping.md` |
| **Airport car parks** (short-stay, multi-storey, long-stay, pre-booked): overstay, unpaid or underpaid tariff | Varies by airport; check the notice | Check the notice | AP1 if the car park is inside the byelaw boundary, plus L4; then the ordinary points (CP1, C6, C1, L5) as secondary. A delayed flight is mitigation (M1), better saved for a landowner complaint than an appeal. A pre-booked parking contract has its own terms: check them |

Airport operators change, so check the NTK rather than assuming.

**The forum's playbook (6500170):**
- **Appeal time still open:** a keeper appeal, saying airport byelaw land isn't relevant land, so there's no keeper liability (AP1). Don't name the driver, and don't tick any online box that identifies who was driving. APCOA and NCP usually cancel at this stage, according to the thread.
- **Appeal time expired (APCOA/NCP):** send the same wording as a **complaint**, not an appeal. APCOA usually backs off. NCP may pass it to BW Legal, whose threats or claims are described as easily defeated while the driver stays unidentified.
- **POPLA:** if the operator issues a POPLA code instead of cancelling, appeal on AP1 and AP2. The thread expects the operator to withdraw.
- **NSL (Stansted):** described as worse, with NTKs said to imply POFA keeper liability that can't apply (AP2). Regulars have complained to the BPA and DVLA about this.
- **VCS / UKPPO (IPC):** one initial appeal as keeper; IAS is usually not worth escalating to (see the IAS note in `arguments.md`). Debt-collector letters can be ignored. A genuine Letter of Claim needs a decision (replying is optional; SKILL.md stage D), and any claim must be defended. A VCS claim is described as defendable; use L3 and L4 with the NS group (`arguments-no-stopping.md`). **The user must keep their address up to date with DVLA and the operator**, so a claim can't go to an old address and become a default CCJ.
- **Any claim:** follow the normal court process (`court-claim-and-defence.md`).
- **Several charges** (e.g. a taxi driver or regular commuter): treat each separately but the same way, and appeal each on time; keep a log of dates and references. If they reach court, they may be bundled into one claim. Get forum help early if there are many.
- **A complaint to the airport** (the landowner) can run alongside the appeal. It has worked at Leeds Bradford for no-stopping charges (6611550, 6558881). Keep it about the operator's conduct and the signs, not who was driving.
- **Apologies and goodwill offers.** Paying is always the user's choice (SKILL.md section 1). But don't mix an apology or an offer of money into a keeper appeal: it reads as an admission and undermines the no-keeper-liability point. If the user wants to pay the drop-off fee (or has already paid it late), that can be done separately; mention a payment in the appeal only as a fact, and only if it doesn't identify the driver.
- **Whatever the story, lead with no keeper liability.** Paid late, paid with the wrong registration, didn't know there was a fee, several charges from repeat drop-offs: the forum's view is that the byelaw keeper appeal (AP1, with L4, and NS10 from `arguments-no-stopping.md` for a drop-off) is still the most reliable route to cancellation at airports. Don't let the facts tempt the user into explaining who was driving or apologising for not paying. Add a fact-specific point (e.g. proof of a late or mistyped payment, CP2; unclear fee signs, C1) only as a short secondary line, and never in a way that identifies the driver.

**Cautions:**
- **Check the byelaw boundary and the byelaws themselves** for that airport (6654021). VCS has replied that some airports' byelaws are obsolete, and an MP has disputed the byelaw argument (6500170). Don't state the land point as certain where the byelaws are in doubt; put the operator to proof. Some airports (e.g. Newcastle) are debated.
- **Grace periods don't help in drop-off zones.** Under the Single Code (Annex B), short-stay areas, including airport drop-off zones, get no grace period, and a minimum consideration period of only 1 minute. Don't claim the general "5 minutes" for a drop-off zone. But 1 minute is a minimum too (C6): if queues, a confusing layout or the time to find and read the payment terms meant the driver needed longer, argue that.
- ***Jopson* (stopping isn't parking) is weak for paid drop-off zones**, because the breach is the unpaid fee, not parking.
- **If the driver has been identified,** AP1 falls away; use the contract and signage points (C1, CP1–CP3) and get forum help.

### AP1. Airport byelaw land: no keeper liability (keeper appeal)
- **Point:** this is L3 and L4 applied to airports. The drop-off zone is airport land under byelaws, so it isn't "relevant land" and POFA keeper liability can never apply. The keeper isn't the driver in law, the driver hasn't been named, and liability can't pass from driver to keeper. So the operator has no one it can hold liable.
- **Applies when:** the location is inside the airport's byelaw boundary; the driver hasn't been identified; the appeal is from the keeper.
- **Evidence:** the incident location and date; the airport's byelaws and boundary map (6654021).
- **Strength:** Strong against APCOA and NCP, which usually cancel (6500170; success reports 6497331 Birmingham, 6503117 Heathrow). Against VCS and UKPPO (IPC) it's usually rejected at appeal and IAS; it does its work if a claim is brought (Moderate–strong in court, depending on that airport's byelaws). *Confidence: Medium* (forum reports, not a court ruling; byelaws vary by airport).
- **Defeated by:** the driver being named; a location outside the byelaw boundary; byelaws that don't cover stopping or parking at that airport.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* the template below.
  - *Complaint (appeal time expired):* the same wording, sent as a complaint.
  - *POPLA:* set out the byelaws, the boundary and L4; add AP2 if the NTK claims POFA liability.
  - *IAS:* as POPLA, with the IAS pre-emptive points.
  - *Defence:* plead L3 and L4 with the airport's byelaws.
- **If the byelaw boundary isn't confirmed** (e.g. a car park on the edge of the airport, or the boundary map isn't to hand): don't assert byelaw land as fact. Use put-to-proof wording instead: "This location appears to be airport land subject to byelaws and therefore not 'relevant land' under Schedule 4 of the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012. You are put to strict proof that it is relevant land; otherwise there is no keeper liability." Check the boundary (6654021) before POPLA.
- **Established wording:** *(keeper appeal; template credited to ChirpyChicken in 6500170)* "This is an appeal by the registered keeper - No driver details will be given. … Due to this being airport land you will be well aware there is no keeper liability due to the byelaws in force and the fact this is not relevant land. As there is no keeper liability then liability cannot flow from the driver to the keeper … Please therefore cancel the notice or issue a POPLA code." The full template, in a more combative tone, is in the thread's opening post. Point the user there, and adapt the tone to the user.
- **Forum threads:** 6500170; 6497331; 6503117; 6654021.

### AP2. The NTK claims POFA keeper liability on land where it can't apply
- **Point:** Single Code cl. 8.1.1(d) says a notice must not "state the keeper is liable under the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 where they cannot be held liable". An NTK for byelaw airport land that claims POFA keeper liability breaches the Code, and it misleads the keeper.
- **Applies when:** the NTK states or implies that POFA keeper liability can apply (e.g. "we may pursue the registered keeper under the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012"), for a location inside the byelaw boundary. Implying statutory authority that doesn't exist is also covered by cl. 8.1.1(a).
- **Evidence:** the NTK wording; the byelaws and boundary map.
- **Strength:** Moderate at POPLA and IAS (a Code breach); supporting in court. Also grounds for a complaint to the trade body (and the DVLA for misuse of keeper data).
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal and POPLA/IAS:* one or two sentences quoting cl. 8.1.1(d) and the NTK's wording.
  - *Defence:* supporting only, alongside AP1.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 6500170 (NSL).
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 8.1.1(d) (`notices-and-codes.md`).
