# Argument library — residential parking and permits

Part of the private-parking-charge-guide argument library (group RS). **Read `arguments.md` first**: its contents list, "How to use" rules, "Gathering evidence" section and IAS note all apply here, and the arguments here often combine with its core groups (L, C, CP, M, CT); C5 (primacy of contract) and C9 (forbidding signs) especially. Argument IDs are stable across files.

## RS — Residential parking and permits

Use when the charge is at the user's home (a block of flats, an estate, student accommodation), or the user was visiting a resident, or the charge is about a permit. Residents' charges are some of the forum's strongest cases: the resident usually has rights that a parking firm's signs can't take away. In the Parking Prankster's summary, residential parking is "quite a minefield … and parking companies are quite likely to get this wrong".

**Before anything else, get the documents.** The answer usually turns on wording:
- **Leaseholder (owner):** the lease, especially the parking clause (often in a schedule of rights), the plan showing the space, and any clause allowing the landlord or management company to make "regulations".
- **Tenant (renting):** the tenancy agreement, and ask the landlord for the **lease** of the flat (or another owner in the block): the tenant's rights flow from it, and leases often grant parking with no permit requirement (6662131).
- **Anything about the parking scheme:** letters announcing it, permit paperwork, anything the user signed. Regulars ask whether the user ever agreed to the scheme; displaying a permit "out of courtesy" isn't agreeing to new terms (6136650, 6624533).

**Playbook:**
- **Complain to the managing agent or landlord (Plan A), in writing, now.** They hired the parking firm and can tell it to cancel. Ask them to show where the lease requires a permit, or allows a third party to charge residents. Ask them to remove the user's bay from the scheme ("opt out"). The forum has template letters for leaseholders to managing agents (5997200). An MP and a CMA complaint are further pressure (6676563, 6355323).
- **Appeal as keeper as usual**, but be aware the operator may already know who lives there.
- **IPC operators:** regulars often advise not bothering with IAS for residential cases and saving the effort for the managing agent and any claim (5598235).
- **If it reaches court,** primacy of contract (RS1) is usually the lead point, with forbidding signs (C9) and signage (C1, C7) behind it.

### RS1. Primacy of contract: the lease or tenancy already gives the right to park
- **Point:** where a resident's lease or tenancy grants a right to park (in a numbered bay, or anywhere designated), that right is part of their property rights. A parking firm hired later by the managing agent can't add new conditions (a permit, display rules, charges) by putting up signs, because the resident's existing contract comes first, and the signs offer them nothing they don't already have, so no new contract is formed. The landlord also can't "derogate from his grant", meaning take away with one hand what the lease gave with the other: in *Saeed v Plustrade* the Court of Appeal held that a parking scheme couldn't substantially cut down a tenant's right to park, even though the lease allowed the landlord to designate parking areas. In *Jopson v Homeguard* (County Court appeal) the judge held that the resident's lease rights took priority over the parking firm's terms, and that *Beavis* doesn't apply to residential parking in the same way.
- **Applies when:** the lease or tenancy grants parking (or a right of way and access) with no permit requirement; the parking scheme was introduced after the lease; the user never agreed to it.
- **Evidence:** the lease (parking clause, schedule of rights, plan); title documents if the space is owned; the tenancy agreement and the head lease; letters introducing the scheme.
- **Defeated by:** a lease that itself requires a permit, or clearly allows the landlord to introduce parking rules **and** third-party charges (read the exact clause; a general power to make "regulations" is often argued not to extend to penalties by a third party, but it's debated, *Medium*); a user who signed up to the scheme's terms.
- **Strength:** Strong where the lease is clear. *Confidence: Medium-high (forum experience; persuasive decisions; Saeed is binding on the derogation principle).*
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal and complaint:* "I have a right to park in [space / the car park] under my lease/tenancy, which contains no requirement to display a permit. Your client cannot vary my lease, and your signs offer me nothing I don't already have."
  - *Defence:* plead the grant, primacy of contract and non-derogation from grant; *Jopson*; *Saeed*.
  - *WS:* lease extracts as exhibits; the date the scheme was introduced; that the user never agreed to it.
- **Established wording:**
  - *(defence)* "The Defendant's vehicle was authorised as per the lease and the Defendant relies on primacy of contract … The Defendant will rely upon the judgments on appeal of HHJ Harris QC in *Jopson v Homeguard Services Ltd* (2016) and of Sir Christopher Slade in *Saeed v Plustrade Ltd* [2001] EWCA Civ 2011." (5978399)
  - *(defence)* "The Claimant's signs offer no new rights or agreements that the Defendant did not already have under the lease. Therefore, no contract can be formed between the Defendant and the Claimant." (forum defence, citing *Link Parking v Ms P*)
  - *(defence)* "There are no terms within the lease requiring lessees or visitors to display parking permits, or to pay penalties to third parties, such as the Claimant, for non-display of same." (6383569)
- **Forum threads:** 5553131 (Jopson, "lease rights beat PPCs at residential car parks"); 5978399; 6624533; 6662131; 6383569; 6682078 (PCM, own bay, tenant asking whether a tenancy gives primacy of contract, 2026).
- **Sources:** `cases.md`: *Saeed v Plustrade*, *Jopson v Homeguard*, *Link Parking v Ms P*, *PACE v Mr N*.

### RS2. The lease can't be varied by a parking scheme
- **Point:** introducing permits and charges is, in effect, changing the terms of residents' leases. A lease can only be varied by agreement, or (for blocks of flats) through the statutory route, under which a tribunal can order variation of several leases only if most leaseholders support it (Landlord and Tenant Act 1987 s.37; forum shorthand: "at least 75% of the leaseholders"). A managing agent hiring a parking firm is neither. Where the managing agent says the scheme "works alongside" the lease, ask it to identify the lease clause that permits third-party charges.
- **Applies when:** the scheme added a permit or display requirement, charges, or restricted bays that the lease didn't.
- **Evidence:** the lease; the managing agent's letters (often revealing: e.g. "introduced because residents breached their lease", 5978399; a breach of the lease is a matter for the landlord, not a parking firm).
- **Strength:** Moderate to strong as support for RS1. *Confidence: Medium on the statutory detail; check the exact s.37 conditions before quoting them.*
- **By stage:** *Complaint:* ask what lease variation was made and how. *Defence:* plead that no variation was made.
- **Forum threads:** 5888512; 6275980; 5015782; 6136650.

### RS3. Permit held but not displayed, or fell off the dashboard
- **Point:** the operator usually accepts the resident is entitled to park; the "breach" is that a permit wasn't visible (it slid off the dash, faded, or was in the wrong window). Where the lease doesn't require display at all, RS1 applies. Where it does, the charge is still disproportionate for an authorised vehicle; the Code's own floor is a reduction to £20 where a valid permit was held but not displayed (F.3(d)), and many operators cancel when shown the permit. Regulars point out that non-adhesive permits are designed to fall down (6662131).
- **Applies when:** a valid permit existed for that car on that date.
- **Evidence:** the permit; any photo showing it fallen in the car; the permit paperwork.
- **Strength:** Strong as a complaint and with RS1; moderate on its own (the £20 floor).
- **By stage:** *Appeal:* send the permit; ask for cancellation (at least F.3(d)). *Complaint:* to the managing agent: an authorised resident is being charged. *Defence:* authorised vehicle; RS1; no loss; penalty (*Jopson*, C3).
- **Forum threads:** 6662131 (VCS, gated development, permit fell out of view; managing agent couldn't get it cancelled); 5812257 (permit fell to the floor; compare a "fluttering ticket" defence); 6604743; 5032084.
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 Annex F.3(d).

### RS4. Tenants, visitors and other people's cars
- **Point:**
  - **Tenants:** a tenant's right to park comes through the landlord's lease; if the lease grants parking without a permit, the tenant has the same protection. Ask the landlord for the lease.
  - **A visitor in the resident's own space:** if the lease doesn't restrict who may use the space, the resident can let a visitor use it; a statement from the resident that they gave permission can help (5553131).
  - **Visitor bays and visitor permits:** read the lease (does it grant visitor parking?) and the scheme's own terms; failed or confusing permit systems (apps, scratch cards) support C1/C8 and a complaint.
- **Applies when:** the driver wasn't the leaseholder.
- **Evidence:** the lease; the resident's statement of permission; the visitor permit system and any failed attempts to use it (6629777).
- **Strength:** Moderate to strong with the lease; weaker for visitor bays the lease doesn't mention.
- **Forum threads:** 5553131; 6629777; 6499026; 5953511 (lost: no evidence the lease had been varied, but the judge wasn't persuaded on the facts; read before relying on visitor arguments).

### RS5. Who authorised the scheme?
- **Point:** the parking firm's authority comes from whoever hired it, usually the managing agent. Did the managing agent have the landlord's (freeholder's) authority to bring in a scheme that charges residents? Put the operator to proof of the full chain of authority: landowner → managing agent → parking firm, with dates and the land covered (L5). Residents' own bays that are demised to them (part of their property) aren't the managing agent's to manage (6355323: "You own the bay").
- **Strength:** Supporting; strong where the bay is demised to the resident.
- **Forum threads:** 6355323; 5032084 (Coupon-mad: the firm should only be able to ticket shared areas, not owned bays).

### RS6. Cautions
- **Counterclaims are a different matter.** Some residents have counterclaimed for nuisance, harassment, data misuse or breach of quiet enjoyment. In one reported win against VCS, the judge said quiet enjoyment was a claim against the landlord, not the parking firm, and didn't accept the data-protection point (6146969). Don't suggest a counterclaim without forum or specialist advice.
- **Multiple charges** at home are common (several a week) and raise the stakes; get the managing agent involved early and ask the forum.
- **Don't pay "to make it stop" without telling the user** that paying may be taken as accepting the scheme; that's their choice (SKILL.md section 7), but explain it.
