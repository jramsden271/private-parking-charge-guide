# Court claim and defence (stages E–F)

Reference file for the private-parking-charge-guide skill. The core rules in SKILL.md still apply — especially: deadlines first, keeper vs driver, statement of truth, confidence labels, never invent.

## Claim form, defence, DQ and mediation (the forum's "8 steps")
**Deadline first.** From the claim form's issue date: deemed service is about 5 days after issue; filing an Acknowledgment of Service (AOS) gives 28 days from service to file a defence — i.e. the defence is due by **4pm, 33 days after the issue date** (forum rule of thumb; confirm on the MCOL claim history). If day 33 falls on a weekend or bank holiday, don't count on it rolling forward — file earlier. Missing it risks a default CCJ. *Confidence: Medium-high.* Filing an AOS and a defence costs the defendant nothing.

- **AOS on MCOL** — done by the **named defendant**, using their own Government Gateway login + the MCOL password on the claim form (never a helper's account). Forum advice: do it no sooner than day 5 after issue and within 14 days of issue (the legal deadline is 14 days after deemed service, about day 19 — compute it, but aim for the earlier date). Without an AOS, the defence itself is due by that date. AOS doesn't end anything — the defence is still needed.
- **Can't use Government Gateway / not online?** The fallback the template thread's checklist gives is to email the signed and dated defence as a PDF to **ClaimResponses.CNBC@justice.gov.uk** (the old CCBCAQ address is closed), and keep the auto-reply. Some recent forum posts report emailed defences going astray, so MCOL is preferred when possible, and the user should check the claim history afterwards. The paper response pack sent with the claim form is another route (its acknowledgment form can be posted; or, if the defence itself is filed within 14 days of service, a separate AOS isn't needed). Send the email **from the defendant's own email account**, and confirm it was logged — without MCOL access, phone the CNBC or ask for written confirmation. A family member can help with the typing, but the AOS/defence must be in the defendant's name. *Confidence: Medium-high (forum checklist) / Medium (paper route and checking).*
- **Defence:** adapt the forum's Template Defence (Post 2 of the template defence thread), filed on MCOL. Keep within MCOL's line limit; check the Claim History to confirm it registered. **Read the Particulars of Claim first.** *CEL v Chan* / *CPMS v Akande* (the template's paragraph 3) are only for Particulars that don't specify the breach at all, or describe it as vaguely as "in breach of the advertised terms and conditions". Judge this on the **Particulars**, not the NTK (a detailed NTK doesn't cure vague Particulars). If the Particulars name the breach (e.g. "no stopping", "overstay"), leave that paragraph out — a one-line "delete the template's Chan paragraph; your claim names the breach" is fine — but don't otherwise bring Chan up unless the user raises it.
**Pulling apart the Particulars of Claim — the core of a good defence.** A strong defence answers *these* Particulars, using the claimant's own words against it, rather than reciting generic points. Ask for the full Particulars (and the NTK, any LBC and any emails from the claimant's side), quote the key phrases back, and work through:
  1. **Who is sued, and on what basis?** Do the Particulars say the defendant *was the driver*, or rely on keeper liability ("and/or pursuant to POFA Sch 4")? Check that against the claimant's own documents: a "Non-PoFA" heading on the NTK, or an email from the operator or solicitor admitting it can't pursue the keeper, flatly contradicts a keeper-liability pleading — and is the claimant's own admission. If POFA is relied on, run the A1/A2 checklist and the relevant-land check (`notices-and-codes.md`) on the incident date.
  2. **How was the contract supposedly formed, and does the breach fit?** Note the exact wording ("accepted by parking", "by entering the car park"). Then test the alleged breach against it: e.g. "accepted by parking" but the breach is "no stopping" — if the vehicle only stopped, on the claimant's own wording no contract was accepted; and if stopping counts as parking, the act needed to accept is itself the breach. A sign that only forbids something ("No stopping") offers no licence or consideration, so it's arguably not a contractual offer at all.
  3. **Do the dates and statutory hooks hold?** Relevant land on the incident date (`notices-and-codes.md` — not retrospective); POFA timings; the claimant's standing (put to strict proof of landowner authority, its dates and scope, and a landowner-set site boundary).
  4. **The money.** Add-ons (£60/£70) as double recovery (*Excel v Wilkinson*); if POFA did apply, keeper liability is capped at the original parking charge (POFA Sch 4 para 4(5)); interest is discretionary.
  5. **Code breaches** — open `notices-and-codes.md` and check the Appeals Charter (Annex F) and notice rules (cl. 8.1.1): e.g. a must-cancel case under F.1, or a POFA claim on a notice that couldn't create keeper liability. The Code binds the operator, not the court, so use these as supporting points.
  6. **Then the supporting points**, briefly and only where they fit the facts: signage prominence and boundary marking, legitimate interest (*Beavis* — distinguish it), Consumer Rights Act 2015 fairness/transparency, *Jopson* for stopping vs parking.
  Open `cases.md` before citing any case. Lead with whichever of 1–3 is strongest for this claim. For the template route, those one or two points are what go in the ~100-word facts paragraph; the template already covers the generic ones. *Confidence: Medium-high (forum approach, from successful defences) / Medium (applying it to a given claim).*
**How much to draft — two routes; lay them out and let the user choose:**
  - **Template + one facts paragraph (the usual route).** The template has room for **one** case-specific paragraph. Draft it **short — no more than about 100 words (roughly 8 MCOL lines)** — so the whole defence stays within MCOL's line limit; count the words and trim before handing it over. Tell the user it replaces the template's own facts paragraph (the place where the template asks for the facts of their case). Pick the one or two strongest points from the Particulars and the facts; don't try to fit everything.
  - **A fully custom defence.** Possible, and sometimes stronger, but say plainly that it's usually not a suitable task for someone new to this with an AI helper: it takes the user's own hours of research to understand and stand behind every point. Forum regulars are generally happy to review defences, but much less so when the whole thing was written by AI for someone who doesn't understand it — and an AI-written defence is easy to spot. If the user goes this way, help them build it point by point in their own words, and keep it plain rather than polished. Keep it focused on these Particulars (generic template blocks aren't required), check it fits MCOL's line limit, or use the template thread's emailed-PDF route if it doesn't (the Government Gateway bullet above, *Medium*).
  Either way, recommend posting the draft on the forum for review, and set a **file-by date about a week before the deadline** so waiting for replies can't run the clock down.
  - **The defence is signed with a statement of truth.** Never write anything the user knows is untrue — e.g. that an expired permit was "valid", or that they weren't driving when they were. "Own words" doesn't override this.
  - **Keeper who *was* the driver:** don't deny it and don't volunteer it. Plead what's true and keeper-neutral: the Claimant is put to proof of who was driving, there's no presumption the keeper drove (*VCS v Edward*, *Excel v Smith*), and — if the NTK failed POFA — no keeper liability. If later asked directly by a judge, answer truthfully (see `hearing-and-ccj.md`). *Confidence: Medium-high (forum practice).*
  - **Keeper who genuinely wasn't the driver:** the default is neutral wording — the Claimant hasn't identified the driver and there's no presumption that the keeper drove (*VCS v Edward*, *Excel v Smith*) — without naming anyone. Saying outright "the Defendant was not the driver" is true and allowed, but invites "then who was?" at a hearing; it's the user's choice, and worth asking the forum. *Confidence: Medium.*
  - **Non-receipt of the NTK:** a defence is signed with a statement of truth, so word it honestly — "the Defendant has no record of receiving…" and put the claimant to strict proof — not "it was never sent". POFA para 9(6) presumes delivery unless the contrary is proved, so this point alone is weak; check with the forum.
  - **A Letter of Claim the user didn't reply to:** say nothing about it in the defence. Replying is recommended but not compulsory, and many people don't. (A reply can pay off, though: the claimant's answer sometimes contains admissions — e.g. about POFA — that the defence can use.)
  - **Errors in the Particulars** (wrong date, location, amount, vehicle): raise them, but not at the expense of stronger points. Occasionally one is decisive — e.g. if the user can show they were nowhere near the car park on the date the Particulars allege.
  - **Expired permit renewed late/backdated** (or similar honest mistakes): mention it briefly in the defence, then expand in the witness statement with the evidence (renewal email, dates). Even if technically in breach, a quick, retrospective renewal is usually seen as an honest mistake. *Confidence: Medium-high (forum view).*
  - **No Letter of Claim before the claim:** that's a breach of the Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims. Mention it as a fact in the defence and ask the forum how to use it (it mainly matters for costs). *Confidence: Medium.*
- **Deciding to pay after a claim is issued:** the amount demanded is usually the full claim total (charge, add-ons, court fee, legal-rep fee); it can be negotiated, but don't promise a reduction (*Low*). Contact the claimant's solicitor **in writing**, agree the amount, pay, and get written confirmation that they will file a **notice of discontinuance**. Keep every court deadline alive (AOS, defence) until the court shows the claim is ended. *Confidence: Medium.*
Then the template thread's eight steps:
1. File the defence on MCOL; confirm it shows in Claim History.
2. Ignore early paperwork from the claimant (their own DQ, "intention to proceed", settlement offers).
3. Wait for the official DQ (N180) from the CNBC; complete the online PDF version (forum's answers are linked from the thread).
4. Mediation is mandatory: a short phone call; offer nothing (or a nominal sum only if a tariff was genuinely owed).
5. Email the DQ to DQ.CNBC@justice.gov.uk (unless counterclaiming) and copy the claimant's solicitor.
6. Don't post things to parking firms by recorded/special delivery; don't use the paper N180.
7. Hearings are rare — claims are often discontinued (very common with DCB Legal; see `whos-who.md`). Ask for your local court; the forum prefers a hearing over a paper decision.
8. Costs and risk are low: no CCJ and no large costs from defending; if you lose, you have time to pay, and the total is usually no more than the claim amount. When comparing "pay now" with "defend", use the actual figures on the claim form (or LBC). If the claimant wins at a hearing, the main extra beyond the claim-form total is the claimant's **hearing fee** (a court fee paid later, so not on the claim form; check the current small-claims fee on gov.uk), plus any further interest; in rare cases a small amount for a witness's travel or lost earnings. And the add-on (usually £70) is often disallowed by judges when it's properly challenged in the defence (*Medium-high*, forum experience) — so the realistic worst case is close to the claim-form figure, sometimes less.
