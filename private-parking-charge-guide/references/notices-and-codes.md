# Notices, relevant land and the industry Code

Reference file for the private-parking-charge-guide skill. The core rules in SKILL.md (disclaimer, confidence labels, keeper vs driver, never invent) still apply.

## Is it "relevant land"? (POFA keeper liability)

POFA keeper liability only works on **"relevant land"**. Land where parking is subject to **statutory control** (e.g. byelaws) or that is provided/controlled by a **traffic authority** is excluded — on that land a private operator can't use POFA to make the keeper liable, whatever the notice says. Check this early: it changes both the advice and the wording of any appeal.

- **Railway land — changed on 26 December 2025.** The Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 (Definition of Relevant Land) (Amendment) Order 2025 (SI 2025/1256, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/1256/made) and the Railway Byelaws Amendment Order 2025 (SI 2025/1258, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/1258/made) brought railway land into "relevant land" from **26 December 2025**. So:
  - **Incident before 26 Dec 2025:** railway land was not relevant land → no keeper liability under POFA; the "not relevant land / railway byelaws" argument applies. The change is **not retrospective** — it doesn't matter when the letters arrived. (Under byelaws, only the person responsible — in practice the driver — could be pursued, through the magistrates' court by the railway, not by keeper liability; *Medium*.) This point doesn't depend on the NTK wording, so a missing back page needn't hold up the reply.
  - **Incident on or after 26 Dec 2025:** railway land *can* be relevant land → POFA keeper liability is possible **if** the NTK is fully compliant. Don't argue "not relevant land"; check the NTK against POFA instead.
  - Always **confirm the incident date from the notice** before advising on a station car park or forecourt.
  - Whatever the date, station complaints go to the **train operating company that runs the station** (or Network Rail for its managed stations). *Confidence: Medium.*
  - **TfL-controlled station car parks:** some regulars argue these may still be excluded because TfL is a traffic authority. Unsettled — *Confidence: Low*; refer to the forum.
  - **Tram / light-rail stop car parks (mainly Manchester Metrolink park-and-ride):** debated on the forum whether these are still non-relevant land after the railway change — tram networks may have their own byelaws rather than the national Railway Byelaws. Unsettled — *Confidence: Low*; don't assert either way, refer to the forum and check recent threads.
- **Airports — the main remaining non-relevant-land category.** Most airports have byelaws, and where those byelaws control parking/stopping/leaving vehicles on airport land, POFA keeper liability can't apply. It depends on the specific airport's byelaws and whether the location is inside the byelaw boundary. Forum list of airport byelaws and boundary maps: https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6654021/the-big-ol-list-of-airport-byelaws-and-plans-maps-charts (a personal compilation — links may be out of date, and some airports, e.g. Newcastle, are debated). Recommend the forum for anything non-standard. *Confidence: Medium.*
- **Other byelaw land** (e.g. some ports, or land with its own local Act): possible, but check with the forum. *Confidence: Low.*

**A "Non-PoFA" heading is a separate point.** If the operator's notice says it is "Non-PoFA" (or omits the POFA wording), the operator isn't relying on keeper liability, so the keeper isn't liable for that notice **regardless of the land**. Use that point — but don't add a "not relevant land" argument unless the land really was excluded on the incident date. A wrong legal point in an appeal weakens it.

## A1. How to check a postal/ANPR NTK against POFA paragraph 9

Use this when no windscreen ticket was issued (the NTK was the first notice). If a windscreen ticket came first, use A2. Go through **every** item, quote **what the notice actually says** for each, and mark it ✅ / ❌ / ? (can't tell — ask for the other side or the missing page). Don't reach a verdict from one side of the notice.

**Quote the statute only from this table.** It is the wording of Schedule 4 para 9 as published on legislation.gov.uk (enacted version; Crown copyright, Open Government Licence). Never paraphrase it inside quotation marks or reconstruct it from memory — misquoting the Act undermines the user's appeal. If you have web access, check the current version at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/9/schedule/4/paragraph/9 for any amendments.

| Para | The notice must (statutory wording) | What to look for / common forum points |
|---|---|---|
| 9(2)(a) | "specify the vehicle, the relevant land on which it was parked and the period of parking to which the notice relates" | A **period** (start and end), not a single timestamp; ANPR entry/exit times are often argued not to be a "period of parking" if they include driving time. The land must be identifiable (e.g. two same-named stores). |
| 9(2)(b) | "inform the keeper that the driver is required to pay parking charges in respect of the specified period of parking and that the parking charges have not been paid in full" | Usually present. |
| 9(2)(c) | "describe the parking charges due from the driver as at the end of that period, the circumstances in which the requirement to pay them arose (including the means by which the requirement was brought to the attention of drivers) and the other facts that made them payable" | Vague descriptions ("unauthorised parking", "breach of terms") without saying which term was broken or how drivers were told (signs). |
| 9(2)(d) | "specify the total amount of those parking charges that are unpaid, as at a time which is— (i) specified in the notice; and (ii) no later than the end of the day before the day on which the notice is either sent by post or, as the case may be, handed to or left at a current address for service for the keeper" | Is there an "as at" date/time for the amount, and is it no later than the day before sending? Often missing. |
| 9(2)(e) | "state that the creditor does not know both the name of the driver and a current address for service for the driver and invite the keeper— (i) to pay the unpaid parking charges; or (ii) if the keeper was not the driver of the vehicle, to notify the creditor of the name of the driver and a current address for service for the driver and to pass the notice on to the driver" | Missing statement that they don't know the driver; missing invitation to **pass the notice on to the driver**. |
| 9(2)(f) | "warn the keeper that if, after the period of 28 days beginning with the day after that on which the notice is given— (i) the amount of the unpaid parking charges specified under paragraph (d) has not been paid in full, and (ii) the creditor does not know both the name of the driver and a current address for service for the driver, the creditor will (if all the applicable conditions under this Schedule are met) have the right to recover from the keeper so much of that amount as remains unpaid" | The **28 days must run from the day after the notice is *given*** (not "from the date of issue/of this notice"); the parenthetical **"(if all the applicable conditions under this Schedule are met)"** is often omitted; the warning must be about recovering from the **keeper**. |
| 9(2)(g) | "inform the keeper of any discount offered for prompt payment and the arrangements for the resolution of disputes or complaints that are available" | Discount and appeal/POPLA/IAS route. |
| 9(2)(h) | "identify the creditor and specify how and to whom payment or notification to the creditor may be made" | Who exactly is the creditor (e.g. several group companies listed)? |
| 9(2)(i) | "specify the date on which the notice is sent (where it is sent by post) or given (in any other case)" | Must be the **date sent**, not just a "date of issue" — a frequently argued point if the two differ or only an issue date is given. |
| 9(4)–(5) | Must be given within "the period of 14 days beginning with the day after that on which the specified period of parking ended" | Count the days. |
| 9(6) | A posted notice "is to be presumed, unless the contrary is proved, to have been delivered … on the second working day after the day on which it is posted" (working days exclude Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays in England and Wales) | Use the date of **sending**, add two working days, check it's within the 14 days. If the notice only gives an issue date, say so. |

**Then:**
- For an anxious user, lead with the verdict and the one or two items that fail; offer the full table rather than pasting it.
- Summarise: which items fail, which are arguable, which pass. Any single clear failure means no keeper liability under POFA (*Confidence: Medium-high* — forum consensus; ultimately a judge's call). Several "arguable" points add weight but aren't decisive alone.
- Remind the user a non-compliant NTK protects the **keeper**, not the driver — so don't tell the operator who was driving.
- **Windscreen ticket first?** Use the para 8 checklist in A2 instead.
- Encourage the user to post the redacted NTK on the forum for a second opinion before relying on a borderline point.

## A2. How to check an NTK that followed a windscreen ticket against POFA paragraph 8

Use this when a windscreen ticket (notice to driver) was issued first and the NTK came later by post. Same method as A1: go through every item, quote what the notice says, mark ✅ / ❌ / ?. **Quote the statute only from this table** (wording as published on legislation.gov.uk; Crown copyright, Open Government Licence).

| Para | The notice must (statutory wording) | What to look for / how it differs from para 9 |
|---|---|---|
| 8(2)(a) | "specify the vehicle, the relevant land on which it was parked and the period of parking to which the notice relates" | As 9(2)(a). |
| 8(2)(b) | "inform the keeper that the driver is required to pay parking charges in respect of the specified period of parking and that the parking charges have not been paid in full" | As 9(2)(b). |
| 8(2)(c) | "state that a notice to driver relating to the specified period of parking has been given and repeat the information in that notice as required by paragraph 7(2)(b), (c) and (f)" | **Para 8 only.** Does the NTK say a windscreen ticket was given, and **repeat** the information the ticket had to contain under para 7 (below): 7(2)(b) "describe those charges, the circumstances in which the requirement arose (including the means by which it was brought to the attention of drivers) and the other facts that made those charges payable"; 7(2)(c) "specify the total amount of the unpaid parking charges relating to that period, as at a time which is— (i) specified in the notice; and (ii) no later than the time specified under paragraph (f)"; 7(2)(f) "specify the time when the notice is given and the date". Compare the NTK line by line with the windscreen ticket if the user has it — a missing time of issue, or a vaguer description of the breach than the ticket gave, are the usual gaps. |
| 8(2)(d) | "if the unpaid parking charges specified in that notice to driver as required by paragraph 7(2)(c) have been paid in part, specify the amount that remains unpaid, as at a time which is— (i) specified in the notice to keeper, and (ii) no later than the end of the day before the day on which the notice is either sent by post or, as the case may be, handed to or left at a current address for service for the keeper (see sub-paragraph (4))" | Only matters if part has been paid. |
| 8(2)(e) | "state that the creditor does not know both the name of the driver and a current address for service for the driver and invite the keeper— (i) to pay the unpaid parking charges; or (ii) if the keeper was not the driver of the vehicle, to notify the creditor of the name of the driver and a current address for service for the driver and to pass the notice on to the driver" | As 9(2)(e). |
| 8(2)(f) | "warn the keeper that if, at the end of the period of 28 days beginning with the day after that on which the notice to keeper is given— (i) the amount of the unpaid parking charges (as specified under paragraph (c) or (d)) has not been paid in full, and (ii) the creditor does not know both the name of the driver and a current address for service for the driver, the creditor will (if all the applicable conditions under this Schedule are met) have the right to recover from the keeper so much of that amount as remains unpaid" | Note the wording differs slightly from 9(2)(f) ("**at the end of** the period … the notice **to keeper** is given"). Same common points: 28 days from the day after the NTK is *given*, not "from the date of this notice"; the "(if all the applicable conditions…)" parenthetical; recovery from the **keeper**. |
| 8(2)(g) | "inform the keeper of any discount offered for prompt payment and the arrangements for the resolution of disputes or complaints that are available" | 8(8) says this includes the creditor's informal appeal procedure and any independent adjudication (POPLA/IAS). |
| 8(2)(h) | "identify the creditor and specify how and to whom payment or notification to the creditor may be made" | As 9(2)(h). |
| 8(2)(i) | "specify the date on which the notice is sent (if it is sent by post) or given (in any other case)" | Date **sent**, not just "date of issue". |
| 8(3) | "The notice must relate only to a single period of parking specified under sub-paragraph (2)(a) (but this does not prevent the giving of separate notices which each specify different parts of a single period of parking)" | One NTK covering several visits fails. |
| 8(4)–(5) | Must be delivered within "the period of 28 days following the period of 28 days beginning with the day after that on which the notice to driver was given" | **Timing is the big para 8 point.** Count from the day after the windscreen ticket: days 1–28 are the first period, so the NTK must be delivered in days 29–56. Too early is as fatal as too late. No NTK at all by day 56 → no keeper liability. |
| 8(6) | A posted notice "is to be presumed, unless the contrary is proved, to have been delivered (and so "given" for the purposes of sub-paragraph (4)) on the second working day after the day on which it is posted; and for this purpose "working day" means any day other than a Saturday, Sunday or a public holiday in England and Wales" | Date sent + two working days must land in days 29–56. |
| 8(7) | "When the notice is given it must be accompanied by any evidence prescribed under paragraph 10." | Para 10 only lets the government prescribe evidence by regulations; as far as is known none have been made, so this is rarely a live point (*Medium* — check the forum before relying on it). |

**The windscreen ticket itself (para 7).** For keeper liability via para 8, the ticket must also meet para 7. Key items:
- 7(2)(a) "specify the vehicle, the relevant land on which it was parked and the period of parking to which the notice relates"; (b)–(c) as quoted above; 7(2)(d) "inform the driver of any discount offered for prompt payment and the arrangements for the resolution of disputes or complaints that are available"; 7(2)(e) "identify the creditor and specify how and to whom payment may be made"; 7(2)(f) "specify the time when the notice is given and the date".
- 7(3): it "must relate only to a single period of parking".
- 7(4): it "must be given— (a) before the vehicle is removed from the relevant land after the end of the period of parking to which the notice relates, and (b) while the vehicle is stationary, by affixing it to the vehicle or by handing it to a person appearing to be in charge of the vehicle."
- Most tickets meet the wording requirements, so forum regulars rarely lead an appeal with "the ticket doesn't comply" — unless there's a real failure. The 7(4) point is the one that matters in practice: a ticket photographed but never actually left on the car, or issued after the car drove off ("drive-away" tickets), isn't a valid notice to driver, so the para 8 route fails — and an NTK arriving from day 29 onwards is far too late for the para 9 route (14 days). (Para 6(2) also says that if a notice to driver has been given, "any subsequent notice to keeper must be given in accordance with paragraph 8".) Look at the operator's photos for whether a ticket is visible on the windscreen. *Confidence: High (quoted text) / Medium (application).*

**Then:** summarise as for A1 (any single clear failure → no keeper liability, *Medium-high*; ultimately a judge's call). *Confidence: High (quoted text) / Medium-high (forum application).*

## A3. How to check a Notice to Hirer against POFA paragraphs 13–14

Use when the user hired or leased the car (rental, PCO/taxi hire, a personal or business lease) and the operator has written to them as **hirer** after the hire or lease firm passed on their details. Hirer liability exists only through paras 13–14. Every condition must be met, or the hirer isn't liable unless they were the driver (and nobody is presumed to be the driver: L4 / L6). Quotes are from POFA 2012 Sch 4. *Confidence: High (statute text).*

**Does it apply at all?**
- It only covers "parking charges incurred in respect of the parking of a vehicle on **relevant land**" (para 13(1)). So hirer liability can't arise on airport byelaw land, on railway land before 26 Dec 2025, or for a stop that wasn't parking (NS10), any more than keeper liability can.
- The car must have been hired "under a hire agreement with a vehicle-hire firm" (13(1)(a)). A "hire agreement" is any letting of a vehicle "for a period of any duration", but **not a hire-purchase agreement** (13(6)(a)). So rentals, PCO hire and personal or business leases count; hire purchase and PCP finance don't (the finance customer is normally the keeper). A "vehicle-hire firm" is anyone hiring out vehicles in the course of a business (13(6)(c)). A company car the employee doesn't hire isn't covered.
- The **keeper** (the hire firm) must first have been given a POFA-compliant NTK within the para 8(4) or 9(4) relevant period (13(1)(b)). If that NTK was late or defective (A1/A2), there's no hirer liability either. Ask for a copy of the NTK sent to the hire firm.

**What the hire firm must give the operator (para 13(2)), within 28 days of the NTK:**
- (a) a statement signed by or for the hire firm that the vehicle was hired to a named person at the time;
- (b) a copy of the hire agreement;
- (c) a copy of a statement of liability **signed by the hirer**, which acknowledges responsibility for parking charges while the vehicle is hired, and gives an address for documents (13(3)).

**The Notice to Hirer (para 14):**
- It must be given within 21 days of the operator receiving the para 13(2) documents (14(2)(a), 14(3)).
- It must come **"together with a copy of the documents mentioned in paragraph 13(2) and the notice to keeper"** (14(2)(a)). In practice this is the most common failure: a Notice to Hirer that arrives without the hire agreement, the signed statement of liability, the hire firm's statement and the NTK doesn't meet the conditions.
- It must (14(5)):
  - (a) say that unpaid charges specified in the NTK may be recovered from the hirer;
  - (b) refer the hirer to the information in the NTK;
  - (c) warn that, if the NTK amount isn't paid in full after 21 days from the day after the notice is given, the creditor will (if requirements are met) be able to recover it from the hirer;
  - (d) give any prompt-payment discount and the dispute arrangements (including any informal appeal and independent appeal; 14(7));
  - (e) identify the creditor and say how and to whom to pay;
  - (f) give the date it was sent or given.
- It must go to the address in the statement of liability, or another address where court documents could properly be served on the hirer (14(6)).
- The hirer can only be pursued after 21 days, and for no more than the unpaid charges specified in the NTK (14(1), 14(2)(b)).

**Hire firm admin fees** (e.g. £30 for passing on details) are a matter between the hirer and the hire firm under the hire contract, not part of the parking charge; the operator can't claim them. Check the hire agreement; if the parking charge is later cancelled, the hirer can ask the firm to refund the fee, but whether it must depends on the contract. *Confidence: Medium.*

**Common forum points:** a lessee or hirer appeals as hirer exactly as a keeper would, without naming the driver. Name the driver only after taking advice. For a company car (not hired), the employer is the keeper, and any naming of the driver is between the employer and the employee.

## The industry Single Code of Practice (BPA/IPC, v1.1)

- **Code rules on the notice itself (Single Code v1.1, cl. 8.1.1–8.2):** the operator must not serve a notice that "a) implies or would cause the recipient to infer statutory authority where none exists; b) deliberately resembles a public authority civil enforcement penalty charge notice; c) uses prohibited terminology as set out in Annex E; or d) state the keeper is liable under the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 where they cannot be held liable." Cl. 8.2.1: "Parking charges must not exceed £100." So an NTK claiming POFA keeper liability on land that wasn't relevant land (e.g. railway land before 26 Dec 2025, byelaw airport land), or a charge above £100, is a Code breach worth adding to a POPLA/IAS appeal. (Cl. 8.2.3 allows the unpaid tariff plus up to £60 where the tariff exceeds the discounted charge, instead of the 8.2.2 discount.) *Confidence: High (Code text).*
- **The Code's Appeals Charter (Annex F) — check it at every first appeal.** Quote these sub-clauses (Single Code v1.1):
  - **F.1 — charges "must not be pursued"** where evidence shows: (a) ambulance, fire, police or coastguard vehicles on operational duty; (b) a doctor or health worker on an emergency call displaying a BMA or Health Emergency badge; (c) a breakdown/recovery vehicle recovering a broken-down vehicle; (d) a vehicle driven onto the land on the instruction of the emergency services, landholder or operator; (e) an unavoidable emergency, e.g. serious illness; (f) a hearse or funeral cortege; (g) a vehicle paused momentarily on a private road network just to check directions (not to drop off, and not in a signed short-stay area); (h) "where the driver has paid the tariff but made a minor keying error when registering their vehicle (for example, 0 instead of o; I instead of L; 1 instead of I)"; (i) "where the parking operator has breached an obligation in this Code relating to the issue of a notice, or such that the driver was unable to adhere to the relevant obligations"; (j) an ANPR/CCTV car park where "payment has been made for the full period of parking prior to the vehicle leaving the car park" (timing of payment only — not underpayment, major keying errors or wrong bays). **No-stopping zones (F.2):** only F.1(a)–(d) apply there, but F.2.1 has its own must-cancel list. The Code defines such zones as "private roads clearly marked with lines and clear, obvious, and repeated traffic-facing 'no stopping' signs and barriers to deter trespass". In them, charges "should be cancelled where there is evidence it occurred in the following circumstances": (a) "to avoid an accident"; (b) "to allow a person to get or give help in respect of an accident or emergency"; (c) "to give way to an emergency vehicle"; (d) "at a closed gate or barrier e.g. at a level crossing" (not to let a passenger in or out); (e) "in recognition of the use of a zebra crossing by pedestrians" (same exception); (f) "due to stationary or slow-queuing traffic preventing the vehicle from moving". Under F.2.3, motorists should provide footage if they have it. The Code also requires the trade body to audit no-stopping sites before charges are issued there. Cl. 5.3 ("Prohibition on stopping") goes further: operators "must only pursue parking charges in instances that could be interpreted as stopping if they have explicit consent to do so on evidenced security or safety grounds from their ATA, following audit of the adequacy of the signs and surface markings", giving "a clear security concern e.g. within airports" as the example (argument NS9). The transition table gives existing sites until 31 Dec 2026 to comply with signage and related clauses; everything else applied from 1 Oct 2024. Annex B gives no-stopping zones a consideration period of "while driving" and no grace period.
  - **F.3 — reduce to £20 for 14 days**, with evidence ("This reduction applies only for the first parking charge issued to the vehicle for the specific contravention, where payment is made within 14 days and where no independent appeal is lodged"): (a) major keying error; (b) onset of illness, an overrunning medical appointment or childcare delay (driver or passenger); (c) breakdown; (d) valid permit held but not displayed; (e) valid Blue Badge held but not displayed, the holder being in the vehicle "whether or not as the driver" (the tariff is still payable) — note this is a £20 reduction, not a cancellation; whether a badge-holder passenger entitled the car to use a disabled bay depends on the site's signs and terms, so don't assert it (*Medium*); (f) payment machines out of order with no alternative; (g) failing to enter the registration into a terminal where the vehicle would otherwise have been permitted.
  - The operator needn't apply the Charter to late appeals (without exceptional reasons), unevidenced appeals, or where there's a history of non-compliance — so appeal on time, with evidence. *Confidence: High (Code text, v1.1).*
- **The Code doesn't trump the law.** It was written by the industry's own trade bodies and binds operators, not motorists. Where it conflicts with contract law, the law wins, and several of its provisions have never been tested in court — so a Code concession (e.g. "£20 is the most we have to reduce it to") is a floor for the operator, not a ceiling on the user's arguments. Use Code breaches as extra ammunition, never as the only possible defence.

### Keying errors

**"I entered the wrong VRM / paid but for the wrong car."** The industry Single Code of Practice (BPA/IPC; see "Keeping current" in SKILL.md) treats keying errors specially (cl. 6.3 and Annex F):
- **Minor keying error → cancel.** The Code's examples: "where the driver has paid the tariff but made a minor keying error when registering their vehicle (for example, 0 instead of o; I instead of L; 1 instead of I)". The operator should cancel at the first appeal.
- **Major keying error → reduce to £20.** The Code lists as mitigating circumstances "warranting a reduction in the amount of the parking charge to £20 for 14 days, subject to appropriate evidence being provided", including "where the driver has paid the tariff but made a major keying error when registering their vehicle for example, letters wrong or missing; characters swapped; motorist entered the wrong car registration (e.g. their previous car or another vehicle from their household)". The reduction "applies only for the first parking charge issued to the vehicle for the specific contravention, where payment is made within 14 days and where no independent appeal is lodged."
- **The boundary matters:** under the Single Code, "minor" means look-alike characters. A single wrong letter that isn't a look-alike (e.g. E for F), a missing character or two swapped characters fall under the **major** examples, so a £20 offer for those is what the Code provides. (The old BPA code called any one wrong character "minor"; a user can still argue it, but say it's weaker — *Medium*.)
- Appeal as keeper to the operator with proof of payment (screenshot/receipt showing the VRM entered), and complain to the landowner too. If the user would be happy to settle at £20 for a major error, they can **ask for the £20 reduction in the first appeal** if it hasn't been offered. See SKILL.md section 7 for the £20 choice. This is the main keying-error definition; other sections refer to it. *Confidence: High (Code wording, v1.1) / Medium (applying it to borderline errors).*


### Grace and consideration periods

**"I only overstayed a few minutes."** The Single Code (v1.1, 17 Feb 2025, cl. 5.2): "A grace period as set out at Annex B to this Code must be allowed by the parking operator in addition to the parking period. A parking charge must not be issued during a Grace Period." There's also a **consideration period** at the start (time to read the signs and decide). The Code's notes limit the grace period: it applies only where the driver otherwise complied with the terms ("it does not apply in isolation so as to allow free parking up to the sum of the consideration and grace periods"); it "does not apply to short stay areas" — controlled land where parking is allowed for no more than 30 minutes, e.g. airport and station drop-off and pick-up zones; and it's relevant only where there's a time limit (e.g. 2 hours free, or 1 hour paid), not where parking is unlimited. Annex B Table B.1 sets the minima: free car parks (1 hour or more) open to the public — consideration 5 minutes (<500 spaces) or 10 (>500), grace 10; pay-and-display and pay-on-departure — consideration 5, grace 10; pre-booked — no consideration period, grace 10; permit areas — consideration 5, grace 10; short stay — consideration 1, grace 0; no stopping — consideration "while driving", no grace period. Remember ANPR times include driving in and out, so a short "overstay" may sit within these periods. *Confidence: High (Code text, v1.1) / Medium (applying it to ANPR times).*

**The consideration periods are minimums, not cut-offs.** Annex B describes its figures as "mandatory minima". Cl. 5.1 requires a consideration period "of appropriate duration", taking into account: (a) the time to find and get to a suitable bay (its note gives examples: a Blue Badge or parent-and-child bay, waiting for a space, checking the registration, queuing at a machine); (b) the time to find and read the signs; (c) the time to find and comply with the payment requirements; (d) the time to leave if the driver doesn't accept the terms; and the effect on (a)–(d) of (e) the layout, (f) the number of vehicles entering and (g) the traffic within the site. Operators must register each site's consideration period with their trade body before issuing charges (B.2), and should keep evidence of how the consideration period ended (Annex B commentary). Against the driver: the period can end early where there's evidence the driver accepted the terms, e.g. by parking and leaving the site, paying, or remaining stationary for more than 5 minutes (cl. 5.1 Note 2; Annex B note). The **CMA's open letter to all private parking operators (16 July 2026, paras 17–23)** says some operators treat the 5 minutes "as a maximum rather than a minimum period", contrary to the Code. It says the period "is a minimum and should not be interpreted as a hard cut-off", and warns against starting the parking period at entry rather than when the driver decided to park. Use with argument C6 in `arguments.md`. The letter states the CMA's view and doesn't bind a court, but it's persuasive. *Confidence: High (Code text; CMA letter) / Medium (how a court will apply it).* Letter: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a5784276d6d5c7e2845ca02/open_letter.pdf
