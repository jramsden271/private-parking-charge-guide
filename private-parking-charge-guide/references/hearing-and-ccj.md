# Hearing and judgment (stages G–H)

Reference file for the private-parking-charge-guide skill. The core rules in SKILL.md still apply.

## G. Hearing listed

**Set expectations, and send the user to the forum.** Hearings are rare: the usual pattern is that the claimant discontinues around the witness-statement stage (often just before its hearing fee or the exchange deadline), so say that as reassurance. Because so few cases get this far, this skill has little detail on hearings. If the user hasn't already got a forum thread for the case, **recommend starting one now** (SKILL.md section 8; offer to draft it), and post the witness statement there for review. Prefer what regulars say in that thread over anything here. *Confidence: Medium-high.*

- **Witness statement + evidence** are required — build it from the defence's points, using the witness-statement format in `arguments.md` (the defence alone is not enough). Deadline is on the hearing notice — usually 14 days before. Email to the **local court** (not the CNBC) and the claimant's solicitor. Have yours ready to file by the deadline (exchange is normally simultaneous); if theirs arrives first, you can respond to it in yours.
- Typical exhibits (choose what fits): the Beavis sign for comparison; photos of poor/missing signs; strike-out orders/appeal judgments; POFA Sch 4 + keeper-liability transcripts; lease/tenancy for own-space disputes; other relevant transcripts (Jopson, PACE v Mr N); the relevant Code of Practice provisions (e.g. grace periods); P&D ticket/receipts; Beavis paras 98, 193 and 198. Add a one-page costs schedule if taking time off work.
- On the day: arrive 30 minutes early, bring printed copies and a crib sheet, decline side-room chats with the claimant's representative, address the judge as "Judge", and ask for costs at the end.
- Late discontinuance: the defendant may apply for costs.
- **Driver at this stage:** a witness statement is signed with a statement of truth — **never say anything untrue**. If the defence is keeper-only, write as the keeper and don't assert facts the user can't truthfully state; if the user was the driver and a judge asks directly, they must answer truthfully. Whether to volunteer it is a tactical call — discuss with the forum before the hearing. *Confidence: Medium.*
- Use the statement-of-truth wording from the forum's witness-statement examples or the court rules — don't reconstruct it. Count "14 days before the hearing" conservatively (as clear days).
- **Strongly recommend** posting on the forum for review of any witness statement.



## H. Judgment / CCJ

Users often arrive here fresh, having never engaged with the parking charge (or never received the paperwork). Start from what the judgment means for them, not from the process.

**What it is.** A County Court Judgment (CCJ) for a parking charge almost always means a court claim was issued and nothing was filed in reply in time, so the claimant got judgment "by default" without a hearing. The commonest reason is that the claim (and everything before it) went to an old address. Say this plainly and calmly; it's fixable, but the clock is running.

**First, get the facts (ask; don't guess):**
- the **judgment date** (on the judgment or the court's letter) → compute the **one-month date** (judgment date + one calendar month), its weekday, and days left;
- the **claimant and its solicitor**, read from the documents (never assume who they are);
- the **amount** and the **claim number**;
- whether the user **received the claim form**, and their address history (did they move? was the V5C up to date?);
- whether they ever filed anything (an Acknowledgment of Service or defence on MCOL).

If they have no documents, they can get details of the judgment from the claimant's solicitor or the court, or search the Register of Judgments (Registry Trust, small fee).

**A practical sequence when there's time and a possible defence:** write to the claimant's solicitor straight away asking them to consent to a set-aside (with the grounds and any evidence), giving a short deadline well before the one-month date. If they agree on acceptable terms, use the consent route; if they refuse or don't reply, the user still has time to choose between applying anyway and paying within the month. Paying first and then applying to set aside is usually a poor mix: payment largely concedes the claim, so ask the forum before doing both.

**The two real options.** Lay out both, with their caveats, and let the user choose; a short table comparing cost, time and outcome works well. Make clear that the set-aside fee is often recoverable from the claimant, or option 1 will look like the obvious choice when it may not be. Don't pad the list with options that don't work; put limitations as caveats under the option they belong to.

1. **Pay in full within one calendar month of the judgment date.** The CCJ is then cancelled and removed from the Register, so it won't affect their credit file.
   - Pay via the claimant's solicitor **in writing** (ask for bank details and written confirmation), then apply to the court for a **certificate of cancellation (form N443)** with proof of payment (gov.uk shows the current fee; it was £19 as of Nov 2026). The Registry Trust says to allow about **4–8 weeks** for the Register and credit files to update.
   - Caveats: paying **after** the month only marks the CCJ "satisfied", and it stays on the Register for six years; paying also ends any chance to defend. Suggest paying a few working days before the deadline.
   - If a lender or broker is involved (e.g. a mortgage), the user may want to tell them early and send the certificate; how lenders treat it is a question for the broker (*Low*).
2. **Apply to set the judgment aside**, then defend the claim.
   - **When it fits:** the user didn't receive the claim (e.g. it went to an old address the claimant had reason to doubt: *Carr v VCS*, `cases.md`), and/or has a real defence. The court **must** set aside a default judgment that was wrongly entered (e.g. an Acknowledgment of Service or defence was filed in time; CPR 13.2); otherwise it **may** set it aside if there's a real prospect of defending or another good reason, and the application was made promptly (CPR 13.3).
   - **An out-of-date logbook (V5C) doesn't rule this out.** Users often blame themselves for not updating it, and agents tend to conclude *Carr* can't help. The forum disagrees: regulars use *Carr* in every old-address set-aside, including where the V5C hadn't been updated (6564529, 6607062), and quote the Court of Appeal as saying a DVLA address is "only a starting point" (6612224). The legal test is whether the claimant had **reason to believe** the address was no longer current and failed to take reasonable steps to check (CPR 6.9(3)); in *Carr* it was phone calls indicating a different address. Things that can count: months between the DVLA lookup and the claim; letters going unanswered; debt-collector traces or returned mail; any contact from the user. Put the claimant to proof of the steps it took. Don't tell the user the set-aside is weak just because the V5C was out of date; say it depends on what the claimant knew and did, and recommend the forum. *Confidence: Medium.*
   - **How:** first ask the claimant's solicitor in writing to **consent** to the set-aside, with a short deadline (about 2 weeks). Then apply to the court on **form N244** with a witness statement about **not receiving the claim and acting promptly once they found out** (not about the parking facts), plus a draft defence. Court fee: gov.uk showed £321 as of Nov 2026; check https://www.gov.uk/county-court-judgments-ccj-for-debt and Help with Fees: https://www.gov.uk/get-help-with-court-fees.
   - **Getting the fee back.** In the application, ask the court to order the claimant to pay the set-aside fee (and any travel and lost-time costs). Where the claim went to an old address, this is often granted: in *Carr v VCS* (Court of Appeal, 2025) the defendant's costs were awarded in full, and forum regulars cite it for exactly this (6612224, 6564529, 5544358). It isn't guaranteed: a judge can refuse, or reserve costs to the later hearing of the claim, and if the claimant then discontinues, the user may have to apply for those costs separately (6498754). Present it as "likely to be recoverable, not certain", so that paying in full doesn't look like the only sensible option.
   - **The cheaper consent route, and its catches.** The claimant may agree to a set-aside "by consent", which uses a lower court fee (about £119–£123 on recent threads; check gov.uk) and avoids a hearing. Regulars are wary of it, for these reasons:
     - **It's usually how the claimant avoids paying your costs.** Consent is typically offered on terms that the user pays the (smaller) fee and gets nothing back, where a contested application would likely have recovered the full fee (FTLA, QDR consent offer).
     - **The claim usually continues.** A consent order just undoes the judgment; the user still has to defend or pay. A contested application in an old-address case can also get the claim **dismissed** for not being served in time, as in *Carr*; consenting gives that up.
     - **The terms can include paying the claim** (6606833). That ends it quickly but is paying in full in all but name.
     - **A late "consent" at the hearing:** if the user has already paid the full fee and the claimant agrees at the last minute, don't let it be waved through without asking for costs ("DO NOT AGREE if the Judge says … the Claimant has agreed … so I don't need to hear from you", Coupon-mad, 6650038).
     - **A later discontinuance can strand the fee** if costs weren't dealt with in the order (FTLA).
     Consent with no costs either way can still be reasonable if the user needs the CCJ gone quickly and cheaply (e.g. a mortgage). Lay out the trade-off; it's the user's call.
   - Caveats: **delay can be fatal**, so act as soon as the user learns of the CCJ; the fee is paid up front and may not come back; if it's set aside, the claim then proceeds and needs defending (`court-claim-and-defence.md`). A consent from the claimant can avoid a contested hearing; whether it changes the court fee, check gov.uk.
- **Recommend the forum** for set-aside cases (link the board: https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/categories/parking-tickets-fines-parking), and give a one-line summary they can post.
- **If the user has lost at a hearing** (not a default judgment): pay in full by the date the court orders, and within one calendar month of the judgment, so the CCJ is removed from the Register. Don't let it go to enforcement. An appeal is only realistic for a clear error of law; ask the forum first.
- **Enforcement:** nothing can be enforced unless the CCJ is left unpaid; bailiffs (High Court enforcement officers) come only after a further court step. If an enforcement letter has already arrived, say so first and act that day.
- **The user genuinely wants to pay**, having understood the facts: respect that; don't argue further. The minimum facts first, briefly: paying within the month removes the CCJ; paying ends any challenge.
