# Argument library

Reference file for the private-parking-charge-guide skill. One entry per argument. First appeals, second-stage appeals (POPLA or IAS), Letter of Claim replies, defences and witness statements all draw on the same arguments, formatted differently at each stage. The core rules in SKILL.md still apply, especially keeper vs driver, the statement of truth, confidence labels and never inventing anything.

## Contents

Argument IDs are stable, so add new arguments at the end of their group rather than renumbering. This file holds the core groups (L, C, CP, M, CT, W) and the rules for using the whole library. Scenario groups live in their own files, marked below. Open a scenario file whenever the facts fit it; a cross-reference to an NS, AP, LS, DS, PY, RS or ST argument means that file.

**L — Liability and standing**
- L1. No keeper liability: the NTK doesn't comply with POFA. *Strong if clear.*
- L2. No keeper liability: the operator's own admission (Non-PoFA notice); at court, an abuse of process if the Particulars still rely on PoFA. *Strong.*
- L3. Not "relevant land" (pre-change railway, airport byelaws). *Strong / unsettled.*
- L4. No presumption that the keeper was the driver. *Strong with L1–L3.*
- L5. No standing: no landowner authority. *Supporting only.*
- L6. No hirer liability: the Notice to Hirer doesn't comply with POFA paras 13–14 (hire, PCO and lease cars). *Strong if documents are missing.*

**C — Contract and signage**
- C1. No contract was formed: inadequate signage or consideration time. *Moderate.*
- C2. The pleaded contract doesn't fit the alleged breach. *Strong when the words conflict.*
- C3. Distinguishing *Beavis*: no legitimate interest, or a penalty. *Supporting only.*
- C4. Stopping isn't parking (*Jopson*). *Moderate.*
- C5. Primacy of contract (residential sites). *Strong with a clear lease.*
- C6. The consideration period was too short for this site: the Code's figures are minimums, not maximums (cl. 5.1; CMA open letter, July 2026). *Moderate–strong for short stays.*
- C7. No adequate entrance sign (Code cl. 3.1.1–3.1.2). *Moderate–strong where missing or unreadable on approach.*
- C8. The charge wasn't prominent: the *Beavis* sign comparison and the "red hand rule" (*Spurling*, *Interfoto*; Code cl. 3.1.3–3.1.4). *Moderate; the standard signage point at court.*
- C9. Forbidding signs: "Permit holders only" / "No parking" / "No parking 22:00–08:00" offers no contract (*PCM v Mr B* and others). *Moderate–strong where the wording is purely prohibitive.*
- C10. Signs not readable in the conditions: dark, too high, obscured, faded (Code cl. 3.1.6–3.1.7). *Moderate with photos.*
- C11. No sign where it mattered: terms only at the machine, none near the bay, unclear boundaries between sites (Code cl. 3.1.3(a), 3.2, 3.3). *Moderate.*
- C12. Unclear, inconsistent or changed signs, including recent changes to terms without the 4-month warning notices (Code cl. 3.4); the operator's photos aren't of the signs as they were. *Supporting–moderate; strong for a regular visitor caught by a recent change.*
- C13. "No return within X hours": the operator can't prove the same driver came back. *Strong for a keeper who hasn't named the driver.*

**NS — No-stopping cases** → in `arguments-no-stopping.md` (airport and station forecourts, private access roads; see *VCS v Ozog* [2026] EWCC 12)
- NS1. A prohibition isn't a contractual offer, unless it comes bundled with something new (e.g. permission to enter). *Moderate where an existing right can be shown.*
- NS2. "Accepted by parking" vs a "no stopping" breach. *Strong when pleaded that way.*
- NS3. Not a properly marked no-stopping zone: no red lines or repeater signs; a "no parking" symbol instead of a clearway sign (Code F.2.1; POPLA Lead Adjudicator). *Strong where they're missing.*
- NS4. Trespass is the landowner's claim, not the operator's. *A passing remark only.*
- NS5. No legitimate interest in penalising a momentary stop. *Supporting.*
- NS6. A stop that wasn't a choice isn't a breach (Ozog; Code F.2.1). *Strong with evidence.*
- NS7. The operator must prove the vehicle passed a sign on the route it actually took (Ozog; Jones). *Strong where a route had no sign; hold the detail back until the WS.*
- NS8. No opportunity to show alternative conduct, so no acceptance by conduct (*Ward*; distinguished in *Ozog*). *Strong where the layout gives no choice.*
- NS9. The site wasn't approved for no-stopping charges (Single Code cl. 5.3, all operators; old IPC Code for earlier incidents). *Supporting only; never lead with it.*
- NS10. A momentary stop isn't "parking", so POFA keeper liability can't attach. *Supporting; part of the keeper-liability package (L1/L4).*

**AP — Airport charges** → in `arguments-airports.md` (drop-off zones, no-stopping roads and car parks; the forum's playbook from thread 6500170)
- AP1. Airport byelaw land: no keeper liability (keeper appeal). *Strong against APCOA/NCP.*
- AP2. The NTK claims POFA keeper liability on land where it can't apply (Code 8.1.1(d)). *Moderate at POPLA/IAS.*

**LS — Left the site** → in `arguments-left-site.md` ("driver and/or passengers must remain on site")
- LS1. No evidence that anyone left the site, or who they were. *Strong where the photos show only the car.*
- LS2. A passenger isn't a party to the contract. *Moderate; untested.*
- LS3. "The site" isn't defined, or the boundary isn't clear. *Moderate–strong at retail parks.*
- LS4. The term is vague or conditional ("may be liable"), so it isn't a clear contractual term. *Supporting.*
- LS5. No legitimate interest: a short visit within the free time, by customers of the park. *Supporting.*

**DS — Disability** → in `arguments-disability.md` (driver or passenger disabled; Blue Badge)
- DS1. Reasonable adjustments: the operator's duties under the Equality Act 2010 (Code cl. 4 commentary). *Moderate; strong as a complaint.*
- DS2. Blue Badge held but not displayed (Code F.3(e); cl. 4.2 signs). *Moderate; often cancelled or reduced.*
- DS3. Accessibility rules on signs (cl. 4.1, 4.2). *Supporting.*

**PY — Payment problems** → in `arguments-payment.md`
- PY1. "Double dip": two visits recorded as one long stay (Code cl. 7.3(d)). *Strong with evidence.*
- PY2. Machine out of order, no workable way to pay (cl. 6.1.2(c); F.3(f)). *Moderate; strong with evidence and no signage.*
- PY3. Paid, but not matched: wrong location code, wrong app, wrong car, system error. *Weak–moderate alone; strong as a complaint.*
- PY4. Paid late, or paid before leaving (F.1(j)). *Strong where F.1(j) applies.*
- PY5. Customer didn't register on the in-store terminal (F.3(g); cl. 3.1 Note 4). *Strong as a complaint.*

**RS — Residential parking and permits** → in `arguments-residential.md`
- RS1. Primacy of contract: the lease or tenancy already gives the right to park (*Jopson*; *Saeed v Plustrade*). *Strong with a clear lease.*
- RS2. The lease can't be varied by a parking scheme (LTA 1987 s.37). *Moderate–strong, with RS1.*
- RS3. Permit held but not displayed, or fell off the dashboard (F.3(d)). *Strong with RS1; moderate alone.*
- RS4. Tenants, visitors and other people's cars. *Moderate–strong with the lease.*
- RS5. Who authorised the scheme? (L5). *Supporting; strong for a demised bay.*
- RS6. Cautions: counterclaims, multiple charges, paying.

**ST — Site types with their own rules** → in `arguments-site-types.md`
- ST1. Hospitals (England): NHS car parking guidance free-parking groups; trusts responsible for contractors. *Strong as a complaint.*
- ST2. Motorway and trunk-road service areas: at least 2 hours free (DfT Circular 01/2022). *Moderate.*
- ST3. Petrol stations and forecourts: queuing for fuel or a car wash isn't parking (CMA investigation, July 2026). *Moderate–strong with a receipt.*
- ST4. Fast-food and "customers only" car parks: complaint to the restaurant first. *Strong as a complaint.*
- ST5. EV charging bays: no contractual sign at the bay, unclear marking, charger failure. *Moderate–strong where signs are missing.*

**CP — Code of Practice points**
- CP1. Grace and consideration periods not given. *Strong for small overstays.*
- CP2. Keying error (minor → cancel; major → £20). *Strong if minor.*
- CP3. Other Code breaches. *Moderate at POPLA/IAS; supporting in court.*
- CP4. A patrol officer watched but didn't intervene: no warning, no windscreen ticket, just a postal charge. *Supporting.*
- CP5. Covert or undisclosed surveillance (hidden cameras, unmarked camera vehicles, covert photography). *Supporting; also an ICO complaint.*

**M — Mitigation**
- M1. Mitigating circumstances and honest mistakes. *Moderate with evidence.*

**CT — Court-stage points**

- CT2. No proper Letter of Claim (Pre-Action Protocol). *Supporting only.*
- CT3. The add-on isn't recoverable (double recovery). *Strong.*
- CT4. Keeper liability is capped at the original charge (POFA para 4(5)). *Moderate, as a fallback.*
- CT5. Interest is discretionary. *Supporting only.*

## How to use this library

1. **Pick only the arguments that fit the facts and documents in front of you.** Use the "Applies when" and "Defeated by" lines. An irrelevant point is noise and weakens the rest (e.g. *Chan* when the Particulars name the breach).
2. **Order by strength.** Lead with the strongest one or two, then add supporting points briefly. Don't bury a winning point.
3. **Format for the stage.** Each entry says how the point looks at each stage:
   - **Appeal** (first appeal to the operator): one or two lines, keeper-only.
   - **POPLA** (second stage for BPA members): fuller, with evidence and quotes.
   - **IAS** (second stage for IPC members): usually the same as POPLA, but see the IAS note below.
   - **LBC reply:** brief reasons for disputing.
   - **Defence:** a denial, or the claimant put to strict proof.
   - **WS** (witness statement): facts in the first person (only what the user can truthfully state) plus exhibits.
4. **Use established wording where an entry gives it.** "Established wording" is phrasing the forum has used and tested. Adapt it to the facts, but don't water it down. Where none is listed yet, write plainly in the user's own words.
5. **Get the facts first.** Check the detail in the fact sources: POFA wording, relevant land and Code text in `notices-and-codes.md`; cases in `cases.md`.
6. **Strength** is a rough guide to how the point usually fares: *Strong / Moderate / Weak / Supporting only*. It's separate from the confidence label you give the user, and it depends on the facts.
7. **Never state a debated (*Low*) point as fact.** Put the other side to proof instead.
8. **Forum threads** are listed by discussion ID. Expand them to `https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/<ID>` when linking, and give the user the specific thread rather than saying "the forum says".
9. **Known sites and playbooks come first.** Where a known-site entry (NS) or the airport playbook (AP) gives the forum's usual lead approach, follow it, then use the strength labels to order the rest.
10. **If the user no longer has a document** (e.g. they threw the NTK away), don't assert what it said. Put the operator to strict proof instead ("the Claimant is put to strict proof that a PoFA-compliant notice was served") and suggest asking the operator for a copy.
11. **Don't show your whole hand early.** Some points work best if the operator doesn't see them coming, because spelling them out tells it what evidence to add. Typical examples: a missing site map or a route with no sign (NS7), gaps in ANPR or CCTV evidence, defects you've spotted in a landowner contract (L5; a plain request for a copy is fine at any stage), missing enclosures in a Notice to Hirer (L6), and route-revealing facts such as a car-park receipt (NS1). Keep any complaint to the landowner general for the same reason. At appeal and in a defence, put the operator to strict proof in general terms; set out the detail in the witness statement and at the hearing. Exceptions: a point that will win at that stage on its own should be made (e.g. a clear POFA failure at appeal), and a defence must still state the facts the user relies on where they differ from the claim (CPR 16.5). It's a judgment call, so suggest checking with the forum. *Confidence: Medium.*
12. **Evidence that could identify someone, at first appeal.** Default: describe it about the vehicle and hold the document back (e.g. "a purchase was made on site at 17:35"; "the vehicle made two visits"). Plain till receipts usually name nobody, so a copy with any card digits removed is normally safe to send when it's the winning evidence (e.g. a double dip, PY1). Documents naming a person (appointment letters, app accounts, order histories, emails) stay back until the user has weighed it up (SKILL.md section 3), or go to the landowner or retailer complaint instead.
13. **Read the whole sign, not just the headline.** Signs often have large, clear headline terms and a block of tiny text at the bottom. Check what the small print contains: the **£60/£70 add-on** (debt recovery or "contractual costs"), camera and data-protection notices, the operator's details, extra conditions. A term in illegible small print is a separate point from the headline terms (C8 prominence; CT3 add-on not incorporated; CP5 camera notices). Ask the user for a close-up photo of the bottom of the sign if they only have a distant one.
14. **If evidence is thin, ask the user to gather it** (see below). Many arguments (signage, layout, lines, sign positions, reading time) are only as good as the photos behind them.

### Gathering evidence: revisit the site, and Google Street View

When an argument depends on what the site looks like and the user has little or no evidence, suggest they get some. Ask before drafting, because the answer can change which arguments to use.
- **Revisit the site and take photos, if they're able to.** Take them from the driver's eye-line along the route in: the approach and any junction or roundabout, each sign (close-up and from a distance), the lines and markings, and the spot where the car stopped or parked. Include wide shots that show the layout. **Cover the route the car actually took**, not just the main entrance: at airports, stations and private road networks, car park exits and internal links often join the restricted road with no sign of their own (NS7). Video of the drive in, from a dashcam or a passenger, shows what a driver sees and how long they have to see it.
- **Stay safe and don't collect another charge.** Don't stop in a no-stopping zone or park somewhere restricted to take photos. Use a passenger or dashcam while driving through, or walk in from public land where that's safe and allowed.
- **Google Street View screenshots** help, especially if a revisit isn't practical. Use Street View's date selector to pick the imagery closest to the incident date. Make sure the capture date ("Image capture: [month year]") is visible in the screenshot, and say in the appeal or statement when it was taken. It's only useful if that date is reasonably close to the incident, since signs and lines change.
- **Note any changes.** If signs or lines have been added or moved since the incident, the current site isn't proof of how it was then; say so. A change made after the incident can itself help the user's case.
- **The operator's own photos** are evidence too, often of what's missing (no sign in shot, no lines). Ask for them, and for any CCTV, early.
- In a witness statement, photos become numbered exhibits, each with the date taken (`hearing-and-ccj.md`).

### IAS note (IPC members)

The forum's general advice is that IAS appeals rarely succeed, so explain this to the user before they spend time on one. Explain the upside too, because it's why some people still try: a standard IAS appeal is free and binds the **operator only** (IAS rules 3.3–3.4), so a win cancels the charge for good, while a loss leaves the user exactly where they were. Never use the paid non-standard appeal, which binds the user too. If they do appeal, IAS assessors often reject keeper-liability points by assuming the keeper was the driver, or by citing *Elliott v Loake*. Arguments that would win at a hearing still fail there. So an IAS appeal should **pre-empt** these:
- the keeper isn't obliged to name the driver, and has not done so;
- there is no presumption in law that the keeper was the driver (*VCS v Edward*; *Excel v Smith*);
- *Elliott v Loake* was a criminal case decided on forensic evidence and is no authority for such a presumption;
- keeper liability exists only through POFA Schedule 4, and only if every condition is met;
- if the operator quotes the Code note saying a refusal to name the driver "may be used as evidence" the keeper drove: the Code binds operators, not the law (see L4).

The IAS lines in the entries below build these in where relevant.

## L — Liability and standing

### L1. No keeper liability: the NTK doesn't comply with POFA
- **Point:** the keeper can only be liable if every POFA Schedule 4 condition is met. This NTK fails at least one, so there's no keeper liability.
- **Applies when:** the driver hasn't been identified, and the NTK fails para 9 (no windscreen ticket) or para 8 (after a windscreen ticket). Common failures:
  - wording: 9(2)(f) "from the date of this notice", or the missing "(if all the applicable conditions…)" parenthetical; no "period of parking"; no date sent;
  - timing: para 9 delivery outside 14 days; para 8 delivery outside days 29–56;
  - a "drive-away" ticket breaks the para 8 route.
- **Evidence:** both sides of the NTK; the incident date; the date sent; any windscreen ticket.
- **Strength:** Strong where a failure is clear; Moderate where it's only arguable.
- **Defeated by:** the driver having been identified (in an appeal, a call or an email); a fully compliant, in-time NTK.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* "As keeper, I am not liable: the notice does not comply with PoFA Schedule 4."
  - *POPLA:* quote each failing sub-paragraph against the NTK's actual wording.
  - *IAS:* as POPLA, plus the four pre-emptive points from the IAS note.
  - *LBC reply:* "No keeper liability: the NTK did not comply with PoFA Schedule 4." If the user doesn't have the NTK: "Please provide a copy of the Notice to Keeper you rely on; keeper liability under PoFA Schedule 4 is denied and you are put to strict proof of compliance."
  - *Defence:* deny keeper liability and put the claimant to strict proof of compliance.
  - *WS:* exhibit the NTK and set out the failures.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet; add forum-tested phrasing with thread IDs)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*
- **Sources:** POFA Sch 4 paras 4, 6, 8, 9 (A1/A2 in `notices-and-codes.md`).

### L2. No keeper liability: the operator's own admission (Non-PoFA notice)
- **Point:** the operator chose not to use POFA, so it can't now pursue the keeper as keeper. A claim pleaded on keeper liability contradicts its own documents.
- **Applies when:** the NTK is headed "Non-PoFA" or leaves out the POFA wording, or the operator or solicitor has admitted in writing that it can't pursue the keeper. This is especially strong when the Particulars plead keeper liability or "and/or pursuant to POFA".
- **Evidence:** the NTK; emails or letters containing the admission.
- **Strength:** Strong.
- **Defeated by:** the driver having been identified.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* point to the heading.
  - *POPLA:* point to the heading, and add Code breach CP3 (cl. 8.1.1(d)) if the notice still asserts keeper liability.
  - *IAS:* as POPLA, plus the IAS pre-emptive points.
  - *LBC reply:* quote the heading.
  - *Defence:* quote the heading or the admission against the Particulars.
  - *WS:* exhibit the documents.
- **Established wording:** *(defence, from a successful defence)* "The Claimant has formally admitted that it cannot transfer liability to the Registered Keeper under Schedule 4 of the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 (PoFA). The Claimant's Notice to Keeper dated [date] was explicitly headed '[heading]'." Then add any written admission, quoted with its date.
- **Escalation: abuse of process (court stage only).** At Letter of Claim stage, just quote the heading against their PoFA claim; save "abuse of process" and costs requests for the defence. If the Particulars (or an earlier Letter of Claim) say or imply that the claim is brought against the keeper under PoFA Schedule 4, while the claimant's own NTK shows it deliberately didn't use PoFA (e.g. a "Non PoFA" heading), say so plainly. The claimant is relying on a law it opted out of, and its solicitor has signed a statement of truth that pleads it. The forum calls this **misleading the court and an abuse of process**. In the defence, quote the heading against the Particulars, call it an abuse of process, and ask for a finding of **unreasonable conduct** with costs under **CPR 27.14(2)(g)**. Keep it factual: "knowingly or negligently", not accusations of dishonesty. Repeat it in the WS with both documents as exhibits. Small-claims costs awards for unreasonable conduct are discretionary and not common, so present this as pressure, not a promised payout. *Confidence: Medium (forum practice).*
  - **Without an explicit heading:** the forum uses the same "misleading the court" wording where **the operator is known never to use PoFA**, typically Smart Parking claims through DCB Legal. The Smart template paragraph 3 (Coupon-mad, 28 Nov 2025) says "Research has proved that this Claimant has never used the provisions of Schedule 4 of the POFA 2012 and they know, or should know, that they cannot hold registered keepers liable"; para 4 adds that the solicitor signatory "is knowingly or negligently misleading the court by citing that law" (6643608, 6627312). That's strongest where the operator or its solicitor has said so in writing (e.g. "we do not currently issue charges under PoFA", 6503827): quote the admission. Check the forum's operator-specific defence threads before using it, because operators change their notices (e.g. Smart's notices before 2025 are described as non-PoFA; later ones may differ).
  - **A one-off non-compliant NTK** (the operator tried to use PoFA but got it wrong: late, missing wording) is different. I haven't seen the forum call that misleading the court; the usual line is "no keeper liability: the NTK fails para 9(2)(x); the claimant is put to strict proof of compliance" (L1). Don't allege abuse of process for it, since the operator may genuinely believe it complied, and an overstated accusation weakens a strong point. The add-on (CT3) can still be called an abuse of process.
  - Also watch for boilerplate Particulars that plead driver "and/or keeper" or "pursuant to PoFA" without saying which; the template's line "liability is denied, whether or not the Claimant is claiming keeper liability" covers this.
- **Established wording:** *(defence, abuse-of-process escalation; from a UKCPS/Moorside Leeds station defence approved by regulars)* "There can be no 'keeper liability' in this case. The original Notice to Keeper was explicitly headed '[heading, e.g. Notice to Keeper (Postal – Non PoFA)]', meaning the Claimant deliberately opted out of Schedule 4 of the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012. Furthermore, the Claimant's own Particulars of Claim contradict their original notice by purporting to rely on PoFA Schedule 4, thereby misleading the court." … "The solicitor signatory of the statement of truth is knowingly or negligently misleading the court and Defendant by citing that law. … This conduct is an abuse of the court process." Pair with a request for costs for unreasonable conduct under CPR 27.14(2)(g).
- **Forum threads:** 6642762 (Smart Parking group defence thread: the 11-paragraph version with this paragraph built in); 6643608, 6627312 (Smart Parking / DCB Legal: "never used PoFA" misleading-the-court paragraph); 6503827 (Smart's written admission); 6637588 (Leeds station, no stopping, UKCPS/Moorside; discontinued); 6664064 (UKCPS/Moorside, Leeds station, "Non PoFA" NTK vs Particulars citing PoFA; defence filed April 2026, settlement offer received June 2026, no outcome yet).
- **Sources:** Code cl. 8.1.1(d); CPR 27.14(2)(g) (costs for unreasonable behaviour in small claims).

### L3. Not "relevant land"
- **Point:** POFA keeper liability doesn't apply on land under statutory control (byelaws) or controlled by a traffic authority.
- **Applies when:**
  - railway land with an incident **before 26 Dec 2025** (the change isn't retrospective);
  - airport land inside the byelaw boundary;
  - possibly TfL station car parks or tram park-and-rides. These are debated (*Low*), so only put the operator to proof.
- **Evidence:** the incident date; the location; the byelaw boundary map (forum airport list 6654021).
- **Strength:** Strong for pre-change railway land and clear airport byelaw land; Weak or unsettled otherwise.
- **Defeated by:** an incident date on or after 26 Dec 2025 at a railway site; a location outside the byelaw boundary; the driver having been identified.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* state the land type and date.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* add the byelaws and the statutory instruments.
  - *LBC reply:* one line.
  - *Defence:* plead it with the date and the statutory instruments.
  - *WS:* add the boundary map or byelaws.
- **Established wording:** *(defence, railway land before 26 Dec 2025)* "The land in question ([station]) is railway land subject to statutory control under the Railway Byelaws. At the material time ([date]) it was not 'relevant land' under PoFA. The amendment bringing railway land within 'relevant land' did not come into force until 26 December 2025 and cannot apply retrospectively."
- **Forum threads:** 6637588; 6654021 (airport byelaws and boundary maps); 6500170 (airport drop-off group thread; see `arguments-airports.md`).
- **Sources:** SI 2025/1256 and 2025/1258; "relevant land" in `notices-and-codes.md`.

### L4. No presumption that the keeper was the driver
- **Watch out: the Code says otherwise.** A note to Single Code v1.1 cl. 8.1 says operators must not presume the keeper was the driver without offering the chance to name them, but "Where the keeper fails or refuses to identify the driver it may be used as evidence they were the driver unless the keeper is able to evidence they were not." Operators and IAS assessors may quote it. Answer: the Code is the operators' own document and binds them, not the court; in law there is no presumption that the keeper drove (*VCS v Edward*; *Excel v Smith*), and keeper liability exists only through POFA.
- **Point:** where there's no keeper liability, the claimant must prove who drove. The law doesn't presume it was the keeper.
- **Applies when:** combined with L1–L3, or where the claim is pleaded against the "driver" with no evidence of who that was.
- **Strength:** Strong in combination with L1–L3; useless alone if POFA applies.
- **Defeated by:** the driver having been identified; a POFA-compliant NTK.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* one line inside the keeper paragraph wherever keeper liability is disputed (L1, L3, L6, NS10, AP1): "There is no presumption in law that the keeper was the driver, and no driver details will be given."
  - *LBC reply:* the same sentence, if a reason is given at all (SKILL.md stage D: one or two brief reasons at most).
  - *POPLA:* one line.
  - *IAS:* essential; use the full IAS pre-emptive points.
  - *Defence:* "The Claimant is put to strict proof that the Defendant was the driver."
  - *WS:* restate it, but never say anything untrue (if the user drove, don't deny it).
- **Established wording:** *(defence)* "There is no presumption in law that the Registered Keeper was the driver (*Excel Parking Services Ltd v Smith* [2017]; *Vehicle Control Services v Edward* [2023])."
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*
- **Sources:** *VCS v Edward* (H0KF6C9C, 2023); *Excel v Smith* (C0DP9C4E, Stockport, 2017). Answer *Elliott v Loake* (a criminal case) if the operator cites it.

### L5. No standing: no landowner authority
- **Point:** an agent operator needs the landowner's authority to make contracts and to sue in its own name. Put it to strict proof of the contract: its dates, its scope, and a site boundary set by the landowner.
- **Applies when:** almost always, as a standard point. It's stronger if the operator's documents are vague or the site has changed hands. For a claimant in administration (e.g. NCP), also put it to proof of the administrators' consent and identify the correct claimant entity.
- **Strength:** Supporting only in most cases. It becomes stronger if the evidence pack lacks a contract, or the contract is redacted or out of date.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* request the contract.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* request it; then use the comments stage to point out if it's missing.
  - *LBC reply:* request a copy.
  - *Defence:* put to strict proof.
  - *WS:* comment on whatever they produced.
- **Established wording:** *(defence)* "DVLA keeper data is only supplied on the basis of prior written landowner authority. The Claimant (an agent) is put to strict proof of its standing to sue and of the terms, scope and dates of the landowner agreement, including the contract, updates, schedules and a map of the site boundary set by the landowner (not an unverified Google Maps aerial view)."
- **Forum threads:** 6637588.
- **Sources:** the forum template appeal and defence.

### L6. No hirer liability: the Notice to Hirer doesn't comply with POFA paras 13–14
- **Point:** the hirer of a rented, PCO-hire or leased car is only liable (if they weren't shown to be the driver) when every condition in paras 13–14 is met. The commonest failure is a Notice to Hirer sent **without copies of the hire agreement, the hirer's signed statement of liability, the hire firm's signed statement and the original NTK**, which para 14(2)(a) requires. Other failures: a late or defective NTK to the hire firm (13(1)(b)); a Notice to Hirer more than 21 days after the operator got the hire documents; missing 14(5) wording; relevant land not engaged (airport byelaw land; railway land before 26 Dec 2025; a stop that wasn't parking, NS10).
- **Applies when:** the user received a "Notice to Hirer" (or a notice saying the keeper named them as hirer), and the driver hasn't been identified.
- **Evidence:** confirm with the user exactly what was in the envelope (what's missing matters); dates; the user's hire agreement; ask the hire firm what it sent the operator and when (a SAR to the hire firm works), and the operator for the NTK it sent the hire firm.
- **Tactics:** the operator has 21 days from receiving the hire firm's documents to serve the Notice to Hirer, so naming the missing enclosures early may just prompt a corrected notice. At first appeal, say there's no hirer liability and put the operator to strict proof of compliance with paras 13–14, without listing what's missing; spell it out at POPLA/IAS or in the defence, when the window has closed (rule 11). On online appeal forms, don't tick any "I was the driver" box.
- **Strength:** Strong where the enclosures are missing or the timings fail; otherwise it depends on L3/NS10. *Confidence: High (statute) / Medium (on the facts).*
- **Defeated by:** the hirer being identified as the driver; a fully compliant Notice to Hirer on relevant land.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* "I am the hirer. No driver details will be given. There is no hirer liability: you are put to strict proof that every condition of PoFA Schedule 4 paragraphs 13 and 14 was met."
  - *POPLA and IAS:* go through A3 point by point; add the IAS pre-emptive points (no presumption the hirer drove).
  - *LBC reply:* "Hirer liability under PoFA Sch 4 paras 13–14 is denied; you are put to strict proof of compliance."
  - *Defence:* deny hirer liability, naming the failed condition; put the claimant to proof of the para 13(2) documents and their dates.
  - *WS:* exhibit the Notice to Hirer and the envelope's contents (or say what was absent), with the dates.
- **Not part of this:** the hire firm's own admin fee. That's under the hire contract (A3).
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet; search "notice to hirer" on the forum)*
- **Sources:** POFA 2012 Sch 4 paras 13–14 (`notices-and-codes.md` A3).

## C — Contract and signage

### C1. No contract was formed: inadequate signage or consideration time
- **Point:** terms only bind if they were prominent and clear enough to be seen and accepted before the driver was bound. Road markings (bay lines, hatching, yellow lines, a disabled or parent-and-child symbol) aren't terms by themselves; they only matter if a sign the driver could read explains what they mean and what the charge is (NS3, DS2). The key term and the charge must be readable from a vehicle on entry. The driver must also have had a consideration period to read the terms and leave.
- **Applies when:** entrance signs are missing, small or high up; the charge is in small print; boundaries are unmarked; signs are only inside the car park; the user was charged within the consideration period.
- **Evidence:** photos of the entrance and the signs (taken at the same time of day if relevant); ANPR times.
- **Strength:** Moderate. It's fact-heavy and depends on the photos.
- **Defeated by:** large, clear signs comparable to *Beavis*.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* brief.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* photos and a comparison with *Beavis*.
  - *Defence:* deny that a contract was formed.
  - *WS:* photos as exhibits, with the *Beavis* sign for comparison.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*
- **Sources:** *Beavis* (on prominence); Code Annex B (consideration periods). For a charge based on time on site, see also C6.

### C2. The pleaded contract doesn't fit the alleged breach
- **Point:** use the claimant's own wording against it. If the Particulars say how the contract was accepted, and the alleged breach couldn't have happened that way, there was no contract on the claimant's own case. For the most common version, see NS2.
- **Applies when:** the Particulars say how the contract was accepted and name a breach that doesn't fit that.
- **Evidence:** the exact words of the Particulars; the sign wording.
- **Strength:** Strong when the words genuinely conflict.
- **By stage:**
  - *Defence:* quote the Particulars and set out the contradiction.
  - *WS:* expand it.
  - Rarely needed at appeal stage.
- **Established wording:** see NS2.
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*

### C3. Distinguishing *Beavis*: no legitimate interest, or a penalty
- **Point:** *Beavis* upheld an £85 charge because of its particular facts: a free retail car park, a legitimate interest in turnover, and very clear signs. Where those facts differ, the charge may be an unenforceable penalty.
- **Applies when:** the site is residential, or paid with no turnover interest; the signs are much poorer than in *Beavis*. For trivial stops, see NS5.
- **Strength:** Supporting only, except on unusual facts.
- **Defeated by:** facts close to *Beavis*. Be candid about this.
- **By stage:**
  - *Defence:* brief.
  - *WS:* cite paras 98, 193 and 198 and show the comparison.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*
- **Sources:** *ParkingEye v Beavis* [2015] UKSC 67.

### C4. Stopping isn't parking
- **Point:** briefly stopping, for example to load, unload or drop off, isn't "parking". If the terms only regulate parking, they weren't triggered.
- **Applies when:** the vehicle was stationary briefly, and the terms or Particulars talk about "parking".
- **Defeated by:** signs whose terms prohibit *stopping* (then use the NS arguments). Also paid drop-off zones, where the breach is non-payment, so this point is weaker.
- **Strength:** Moderate, as the case is persuasive only.
- **By stage:** used at every stage. In a defence, pair it with NS2.
- **Established wording:** *(defence)* "Stopping is not parking (*Homeguard Services Ltd v Jopson* [2016]). The vehicle was stationary for [duration]."
- **Forum threads:** 6637588.
- **Sources:** *Jopson v Homeguard* (2016).

### C5. Primacy of contract (residential sites)
- **Point:** a lease or tenancy that grants a right to park can't be cut down by a parking firm's later signs. **For any residential case, open `arguments-residential.md`** (RS1 has the full argument, cases and wording).
- **Applies when:** the user lives there, or is visiting a resident, and the lease or tenancy grants parking rights.
- **Evidence:** the lease or tenancy agreement (the parking clause); the allocated space.
- **Strength:** Strong where the lease is clear. Clauses allowing "reasonable regulations" by the landlord are debated (*Low*).
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* also complain to the managing agent.
  - *Defence:* plead the grant.
  - *WS:* lease extract as an exhibit.

### C6. The consideration period was too short for this site: the Code's figures are minimums, not maximums
- **Point:** a driver can't accept terms they haven't had a fair chance to find, read and decide on, or to reject by leaving. The Single Code's consideration periods (Annex B: 5 minutes at most sites) are **mandatory minimums**, not a cut-off after which the driver is bound. Cl. 5.1 says the period must be "of appropriate duration" and lists what it must allow for:
  - finding a suitable bay (e.g. a Blue Badge or parent-and-child bay, waiting for a space, queuing at a machine);
  - finding and reading the signs;
  - working out how to pay, and paying;
  - leaving if the driver decides not to accept the terms;
  - the effect of the site's layout, the number of vehicles entering, and the traffic inside.

  On 16 July 2026 the CMA's open letter to all private parking operators said the same. Some operators were treating the 5 minutes "as a maximum rather than a minimum period", contrary to the Industry Code. The CMA said the period "is a minimum and should not be interpreted as a hard cut-off", and that operators should apply the cl. 5.1 factors. **So exceeding the operator's consideration period doesn't, on its own, prove acceptance or a breach.** Argue that at this site, on this day, the time actually needed was longer.
- **Applies when:**
  - the charge rests on time on site (ANPR entry and exit, or the driver "remained" too long), e.g. a driver who entered, looked for a space or a sign, decided not to stay and left after 5–11 minutes;
  - the site is large, busy or confusing; there were queues, a full car park or no suitable bay; the signs were hard to find or read; the payment machine or app failed; or the exit was slow;
  - an overstay is short, and ANPR times include the time to drive in, find a space, park, walk to a sign, read it and decide (6673702).
- **Also:** for an overstay of paid or free time, the CMA says operators may wrongly start the parking period at entry rather than when the driver decided to park, so the driver doesn't get the full period they paid for or were allowed. Combine with CP1 (grace period) and put the operator to proof of when the parking period actually started.
- **Evidence:** a timeline of what the driver did, minute by minute; photos of the site layout, the sign positions and the distance from entrance to signs (see "Gathering evidence"); evidence of queues, a full car park, a broken machine or no phone signal (screenshots, receipts, app logs); the operator's own ANPR images.
- **Put the operator to proof of:** the consideration period it registered with its trade body for this site (Annex B.2 requires registration before issuing charges); and the evidence it relies on to say the consideration period had ended (the Annex B commentary says operators "should retain evidence to show how the consideration period had ended").
- **Defeated by:** clear evidence the driver accepted the terms early, which the Code says can end the consideration period before the minimum. Its examples are parking and leaving the site, paying, or remaining stationary for more than 5 minutes (cl. 5.1 Note 2; Annex B note). Queuing to leave is arguably not "remaining stationary" by choice, but it's untested; don't state the stationary time as fact unless the evidence shows it, and put the operator to proof of how its consideration period ended. Note the 10-minute figure for sites over 500 spaces in Annex B is for **free** car parks only; pay sites get 5. Short-stay sites (30 minutes or less, e.g. petrol stations, drop-off zones) get a minimum of only 1 minute and no grace period; the "minimum, not a cut-off" argument still applies, but it has to rest on the actual time needed (queues, layout, cl. 5.1(e)–(g)). If the driver parked and walked off to a shop, this point doesn't help; use other points. Also defeated by a stay far longer than any realistic consideration time.
- **Strength:** Moderate to strong where the stay was short and the driver left without parking, or the site was busy or confusing; supporting otherwise. *Confidence: Medium*: the CMA letter states the CMA's view of the Code and consumer law, and doesn't bind a court. It's persuasive and recent, and it carries weight with operators, POPLA and IAS.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* the driver entered, looked for [a space / the signs], decided not to accept the terms and left; the Code's 5 minutes is a minimum, not a cut-off (CMA open letter, 16 July 2026).
  - *POPLA and IAS:* set out the cl. 5.1 factors that applied, the timeline and photos, and the CMA letter (paras 17–23). As keeper, say the driver stopped to read the signs, rejected the offer and left (6679354).
  - *Defence:* no contract was formed within the time on site; the claimant is put to strict proof of the actual period of parking, the timekeeping method, and when the consideration period ended.
  - *WS:* the timeline in the first person, with exhibits and the CMA letter.
- **Established wording:**
  - *(WS, Coupon-mad, 6673702)* relying only on ANPR entry and exit times, with "a bare ten minute grace period at the end", "fails to account for the time also needed ('consideration period')" to drive in, find a space, park, gather belongings, walk to the nearest sign, read it, and decide to stay or leave.
  - *(defence, 6673702)* "The Claimant is put to strict proof of: (a) the actual period of parking; (b) the method of timekeeping relied upon … (c) that the Defendant's vehicle was parked for longer than any permitted period."
- **Also useful:** a complaint to the CMA with evidence about the operator. The CMA doesn't handle individual cases, but it collects evidence about firms (6679354).
- **Forum threads:** 6679354 (Euro Parking Services, a 3m 46s stay; Coupon-mad: "Three minutes is predatory"; IAS appeal citing the CMA letter, Aug 2026); 6673702 (ANPR "total stay" and the consideration period in a WS, July 2026); 6679952 (CEL defence citing the CMA letter); 6482843 (PE, 5m 4s, under the old BPA Code).
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 2.9, cl. 5.1 (with Note 2), the Annex B commentary, B.1, B.2 (`notices-and-codes.md`); CMA open letter to private parking operators, 16 July 2026, paras 17–23 and Annex A: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a5784276d6d5c7e2845ca02/open_letter.pdf ; CMA letter to MHCLG, 16 July 2026 (recommendations on the consideration period): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cma-letter-to-government-in-relation-to-private-parking .

### C7. No adequate entrance sign
- **Point:** the Code requires an entrance sign at each vehicle entrance (cl. 3.1.1, with limited exceptions) that tells drivers terms and conditions apply, whether payment is required or free parking is time-limited, the operator's name and trade-body logo, and whether cash is taken (cl. 3.1.2(a)–(e)). It "must not imply an invitation to park where public parking is not permitted", and its "size and positioning … must take into account the expected speed and direction of travel of vehicles approaching the entrance and must be visible (i.e. not be obscured e.g. by foliage or other objects)". Where parking is invited after dark, entrance signs "must be visible to approaching drivers and hence may require lighting" (Note 1). Without a proper entrance sign, a driver has no warning that terms apply before committing to enter.
- **Applies when:** no entrance sign on the route used; a sign only saying something vague ("restrictions apply, read signs within"); small, sideways-on, obscured or unlit at the entrance; several entrances with only one signed.
- **Evidence:** photos or video of every entrance from the driver's position, at the same time of day (see "Gathering evidence"); dated Street View.
- **Strength:** Moderate to strong where there's no entrance sign at all on the route used, or it's unreadable on approach. Supporting where an entrance sign exists but is merely small.
- **By stage:** *Appeal:* one line. *POPLA and IAS:* cite cl. 3.1.1–3.1.2 with photos. *Defence:* put the claimant to proof of an entrance sign meeting the Code on the route the vehicle took. *WS:* photos as exhibits.
- **Established wording:** *(WS, from a forum statement)* "If you approach the car park from this direction, there was only a single sign visible to me at the entrance to the car park … It states only that 'PARKING RESTRICTIONS APPLY' and to 'READ SIGNAGE WITHIN'." (6054571)
- **Forum threads:** 6054571; 6017649 (a sign facing sideways about 12 feet up, "barely … noticeable or read even if one is standing underneath it").
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 3.1.1, 3.1.2 and Note 1; Annex A.

### C8. The charge wasn't prominent: the *Beavis* comparison and the "red hand rule"
- **Point:** a term only binds if it was fairly brought to the driver's attention before the contract was made, and the more onerous or unusual the term, the more prominent it must be. A £100 charge for a minor breach is the most onerous term on the sign. Lord Denning's "red hand rule": some clauses "would need to be printed in red ink on the face of the document with a red hand pointing to it before the notice could be held to be sufficient" (*Spurling v Bradshaw*; applied in *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking* and *Interfoto v Stiletto*). *Beavis* itself turned on unusually clear signs: large, prominent, at the entrance and throughout, with the charge in large text. The standard forum approach is to put the *Beavis* sign side by side with the site's signs and invite the court to compare them. The Code backs this up: terms signs must be "sufficiently large to be visible from a distance and legible on approach" and "display the parking charge … in a large font" (cl. 3.1.3(b), (j)), and the charge must be shown "in a font of comparable size and boldness to the main body text" and no smaller than any tariff text (cl. 3.1.4).
- **Small print below prominent headline terms:** even where the main terms are large and clear, check the small text at the bottom. The add-on fee, data-protection and camera notices are often there, too small to read from a car or from a normal distance. Those terms aren't fairly brought to attention (*Spurling*, *Interfoto*) even if the headline terms are; argue it for each term separately.
- **Applies when:** the charge is in small print, buried in a block of text, smaller than the tariff or headline, or only readable close up; the signs are wordy, cluttered or low-contrast (cl. 3.1.3(e)–(g)).
- **Evidence:** close-up and distance photos showing the charge text; a measurement or estimate of text height; the *Beavis* sign (from the judgment, or the copy linked in the forum's newbies and template threads) as an exhibit for comparison.
- **Strength:** Moderate. It's a fixture of forum witness statements and judges do find against unclear signs, but against large, clear signs it fails (*Beavis*).
- **Defeated by:** signs comparable to *Beavis*; a driver who admits reading the terms.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* one line ("the charge was not prominently displayed").
  - *POPLA and IAS:* photos plus the Code clauses.
  - *Defence:* deny that the charge was incorporated; it was not prominent (*Spurling*, *Interfoto*, *Beavis* distinguished).
  - *WS:* the side-by-side comparison as an exhibit; describe what a driver could and couldn't read from where they were.
- **Established wording:**
  - *(WS, adapted from several forum statements, e.g. 6054571)* "I have included a copy of the sign from *ParkingEye v Beavis* in exhibit [x] for comparison. That sign was clear and prominent, with the charge in large lettering. In this case, the signage fails to meet that standard."
  - *(defence/WS)* "The 'red hand rule' from *Spurling v Bradshaw* [1956] 1 WLR 461 further supports that any onerous or unusual terms (such as penalty charges) must be made extremely prominent, which they were not in this instance." (6575502)
- **Forum threads:** 5707549 (Beavis sign comparison and red hand rule); 6054571; 6210723; 6575502; 6305625.
- **Sources:** `cases.md`: *Beavis*, *Spurling v Bradshaw*, *Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking*, *Interfoto v Stiletto*; Single Code v1.1 cl. 3.1.3(b), (e)–(g), (j), 3.1.4.

### C9. Forbidding signs: "Permit holders only" or "No parking" offers no contract
- **Point:** a contract needs an offer. A sign that only forbids ("No parking at any time", "Permit holders only", "Residents only") offers nothing to anyone outside the permitted group, so a driver who parks there can't have accepted an offer, and there's no contract to breach. At most it's a trespass, which only the landowner (or someone in possession) can sue for, and only for actual loss. In *PCM (UK) v Mr B*, *Mr W* and *Ms L* (2016) the District Judge found a "No Parking At Any Time" sign "absolutely prohibitive", communicating no offer of parking, and dismissed all three claims; *Beavis* didn't apply because there the contract was agreed. This is the same principle as NS1 for no-stopping signs.
- **Applies when:** the sign's main message is a prohibition, and the charge is presented as a contractual sum for doing the forbidden thing; typically residential sites, private roads, permit-only areas.
- **Special case: no parking out of hours.** Common at retail parks: "No parking between 22:00 and 08:00" (or "Car park closed"). During those hours the sign offers no parking at all, so a car there overnight hasn't accepted any offer; it's a prohibition for that period, and at most a trespass. Check the exact wording for each period separately: the daytime terms may well be a valid offer, but they don't cover the night. Check the timings too: if the car arrived in the permitted hours and stayed into the closed period, the charge may be framed as an overstay of the daytime terms instead (then C8/C10 on how clearly the closing time was shown, and CP1). If the charge is pleaded as "parked during prohibited hours" for a car that arrived in permitted hours: the only offer the driver accepted was the daytime one, which says nothing about the night, and the night-time sign offers nothing at all; so there's no contractual term covering the night to breach, at most a trespass (put the operator to proof of which term was breached and when).
  - **Defeated by:** sites that offer short parking out of hours instead of forbidding it (e.g. "08:00–22:00: 3 hours; 22:00–08:00: 10 minutes"). That's an offer with a time limit, so an overnight stay is an ordinary overstay; use the overstay points, plus C10 (were the signs readable at night?).
- **Wording like "Permit holders only. Unauthorised vehicles will be issued a £100 PCN"** is still treated on the forum as forbidding: it threatens a charge but offers non-permit holders no right to park (compare the UKCPS example on FTLA). *Moderate*; read the whole sign.
- **Defeated by:** signs that do offer parking to everyone on stated terms (e.g. "Parking for non-permit holders is £100 per day", or "By parking without a permit you agree to pay £100"). Some judges treat such wording as a conditional offer, so read the exact words; the claimant will argue it. Also defeated where the driver *was* a permit holder who forgot to display (then it's a permit/terms point, and C5 primacy of contract may be stronger).
- **Strength:** Moderate to strong where the wording is purely prohibitive. First-instance decisions only, so persuasive. *Confidence: Medium.*
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal and POPLA/IAS:* quote the sign and say it makes no offer.
  - *Defence:* "The signs are forbidding and incapable of forming a contract with a driver who was not a permit holder; at most this is a trespass, a matter for the landowner." Cite the PCM cases.
  - *WS:* exhibit the sign; quote the PCM reasoning; contrast it with a sign that does offer terms.
- **Established wording:** *(defence, widely used)* "It is limited to cars displaying a valid permit only and therefore the terms cannot apply to cars without a permit because the signage does not offer an invitation to park on certain terms. The terms are forbidding." (6316993) Also "it is trite law that a term that is forbidding cannot also constitute an offer" (6270450).
- **Forum threads:** 6680804 (a sign that "expressly does not offer parking at all after 22:30": no contract offered for that period, at most trespass); 5451157 (Coupon-mad: "Permit Holders Only" is "the same as saying to such a person 'No Parking'"); 5825835; 6316993; 6270450; 6097447.
- **Sources:** `cases.md`: *PCM (UK) v Mr B, Mr W, Ms L*; NS1 for the no-stopping version.

### C10. Signs not readable in the conditions: dark, too high, obscured, faded
- **Point:** signs "must be designed and installed so as to be conspicuous and legible in all lighting conditions during which the controlled land may legitimately be accessed, at a height that takes account of whether the signs are intended to be viewed from the vehicle (including by headlight in the hours of darkness)" (cl. 3.1.6). Lighting may be needed "to meet the test of prominence in the Consumer Rights Act 2015" (Note 1). Signs must stay legible despite weathering, sunlight, dirt or "growth of foliage" (cl. 3.1.7). A sign the driver couldn't read when they parked can't have formed a contract.
- **Applies when:** the parking was after dark and the signs are unlit; signs are high up or angled away; hidden by trees, vans or other signs; faded or dirty.
- **Evidence:** photos taken at the same time of day (and in the dark, for a night-time event), from the driver's position; note the height; the operator's own photos (often taken in daylight or with flash).
- **Strength:** Moderate with good photos; strong for a night-time event with unlit signs.
- **By stage:** *Appeal:* one line. *POPLA and IAS:* cl. 3.1.6–3.1.7 with photos. *Defence and WS:* the conditions at the time, with exhibits.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 6017649.
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 3.1.6, 3.1.7.

### C11. No sign where it mattered
- **Point:** terms signs must be "placed within the controlled land, such that drivers have the chance to read them at the time of parking or leaving their vehicle" (cl. 3.1.3(a)). If the only terms were on a pay machine across the car park, or nowhere near where the car parked, the driver had no fair chance to read them. Where adjoining areas have different terms and no physical separation, signs or markings must "delineate clearly between these premises" (cl. 3.2); compare LS3. Where the breach is not parking within a bay, the bay markings must be "clearly visible to drivers in all lighting and weather conditions", and clear in the photos (cl. 3.3). And markings alone aren't terms: they only matter if a sign explains them (C1).
- **Applies when:** no terms sign near the bay or walking route; terms only on machines; shared or adjoining sites with unclear boundaries; faded or missing bay lines.
- **Evidence:** a site plan or sketch with sign positions and where the car was; photos along the walking route.
- **Strength:** Moderate.
- **By stage:** *POPLA and IAS:* plan plus photos and the clauses. *Defence:* put to proof of a sign near where the vehicle was parked. *WS:* plan and photos.
- **Established wording:** *(WS)* "At the point of entry, the entrance terms and conditions sign is not visible or readable … The only viewable signage is the pay and display machine sign." (forum WS template, Google Docs exhibit list)
- **Forum threads:** *(add as found)*
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 3.1.3(a), 3.2, 3.3.

### C12. Unclear, inconsistent or changed signs; the operator's photos aren't of the signs as they were
- **Point:** several smaller points that often add up:
  - **Unclear or contradictory wording.** Ambiguous terms are read in the consumer's favour (CRA 2015 s.69). Examples: "Permit holders only" next to "Visitors parking"; different charges on different signs; "may be liable" (LS4). All signs on a site should be consistent in typeface, size and layout (cl. 3.1.6 Note 2).
  - **"Penalty" or "fine".** Signs must not use those words unless required by statute (cl. 3.1.3(i)).
  - **Terms changed recently, without the required warning notices.** Where there's "any material change to any pre-existing terms and conditions that would not be immediately apparent to a driver" at a site that is or has been open for public parking, the operator "must place additional (temporary) notices at the site entrance for a period of not less than 4 months from the date of the change making it clear that new terms and conditions/charges apply, such that regular visitors who might be familiar with the old terms do not inadvertently incur parking charges" (Single Code v1.1 cl. 3.4). The Code's examples: enforcement introduced where there was none, time-limited free parking introduced, or the free period shortened. Repeater notices may also be needed on larger sites. This is strong for regular visitors caught out by a change: put the operator to proof of the date of the change and of the temporary entrance notices for the following 4 months. Evidence of when things changed: dated Street View, store staff, local news or online reviews mentioning the new rules.
  - **Signs changed since the incident.** Operators' evidence packs sometimes show signs photographed on another date, or a generic sign. Put them to proof of the signs in place on the incident date (dated photos, installation records). The user's own dated photos, or dated Street View, can show what was there (or wasn't). A forum WS warned of a claimant "appending false signage to their witness statement that does not exist at this car park".
- **Strength:** Supporting to moderate; the "signs as at the date" point can be strong where the operator's photos are clearly from another time, and the cl. 3.4 point is strong for a regular visitor caught out by a recent change.
- **By stage:** *POPLA and IAS:* ask for dated photos of the signs as at the incident date. *Defence:* put to proof. *WS:* compare their photos with yours, with dates.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 4488337 (POPLA decisions thread: "permit holder only" beside "visitors parking only" argued as ambiguous).
- **Sources:** CRA 2015 s.69; Single Code v1.1 cl. 3.1.3(i), 3.1.6 Note 2, 3.4.

### C13. "No return within X hours": the operator can't prove the same driver came back
- **Point:** a "no return within [60 minutes / 2 hours]" term is a term of the contract made by the driver on a visit. It can only be broken if **the same driver** came back within the period. A second visit by a different driver is a new contract with a new person, who isn't bound by what the first driver agreed. ANPR records a number plate, not who was driving, so on its own it can't show the same person drove both times. Unless the keeper identifies the driver(s), the operator has no evidence of a breach. A contract "is with a person, not with a lump of metal" (FTLA, 2025).
- **Keeper liability too:** POFA keeper liability rests on a notice about "the period of parking" (Sch 4 para 9(2)(a)). A "no return" allegation by definition spans two visits, so regulars argue the notice can't specify a single period of parking for it (*Medium*; supporting, alongside L1).
- **Applies when:** the charge is for returning too soon; the driver(s) haven't been identified.
- **Defeated by:** the keeper naming the driver, or saying "I came back" in an appeal; other evidence identifying the same person both times. So the keeper must not explain the two visits in a way that identifies who drove them.
- **Strength:** Strong for a keeper who hasn't named the driver. *Confidence: Medium-high (forum reasoning; no reported court decision that we've found).*
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal (keeper):* "A 'no return' term can only be breached by the same driver returning. ANPR identifies a vehicle, not a driver, and you have no evidence that the same person drove on both visits. No driver details will be given." Add L1/L4.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* the same, plus the para 9(2)(a) point and the IAS pre-emptive points.
  - *Defence:* the claimant is put to strict proof that the same individual drove on both occasions; keeper liability denied.
  - *WS:* if true, the keeper can state that they don't know or haven't said who drove each time; only state that different people drove if that's true and the user has decided to say it (SKILL.md section 3).
- **Established wording:** *(FTLA, keeper appeal)* "ANPR simply records a plate passing a camera. It does not and cannot identify who was driving, and it certainly does not prove that the same person drove on both visits."
- **Forum threads:** FTLA "Returning within a no return period – ParkdirectUK" (2025); see also PY1 (a double dip looks the same on camera).

## CP — Code of Practice points (support, not the whole case)

The Single Code binds operators, not motorists, and doesn't override the law. Use breaches as extra weight. A Code concession (e.g. £20) is the least the operator has to do, not a limit on the user's arguments.

### CP1. Grace and consideration periods not given
- **Point:** the Code requires a consideration period at the start and a grace period at the end. A charge must not be issued during a grace period (cl. 5.2). The Annex B figures are minimums; where the time on site exceeded the operator's consideration period but the site needed longer, use C6.
- **Applies when:** the overstay or short stay falls within those periods. ANPR times include driving in and out.
- **Defeated by:** short-stay and no-stopping areas (no grace period); car parks with no time limit; non-compliance with the terms other than the time limit (the grace period doesn't apply in isolation).
- **How to do the arithmetic:** for an ANPR stay, compare the total time on site with: the permitted time + the minimum consideration period at entry + the grace period at the end (e.g. 90 min free + 5 + 10 = 105 minutes). Expect pushback: operators (and some advisers) say the consideration period ends when the driver parks, so it can't be added to the grace period, and the Code says the grace period doesn't create free parking "up to the sum of the consideration and grace periods" (cl. 5.2 Note 1). The answer is that the parking period starts when the driver decided to park, not at camera entry (CMA open letter, para 22; C6), so the time spent driving in and finding a space isn't part of it. ANPR times also include driving in, finding a space and queuing out. Within that total: strong. A minute or two over: still arguable, but lead with C6 (the minimum isn't a cut-off; time needed to drive in and out) rather than claiming a clear Code breach. Note C6 is weak where the driver plainly parked and went in (that ends the consideration period, cl. 5.1 Note 2), so for a customer who parked, being several minutes over the total is only a supporting point: put the operator to proof of its timings and lead with other points. Well over: weak.
- **Strength:** Strong for small overstays within the Annex B figures; Moderate just over them (with C6); Weak otherwise.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* brief.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* cite cl. 5.2 and the Annex B row for the site.
  - *Defence and WS:* cite as supporting.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*
- **Older incidents (before 1 Oct 2024):** the old BPA and IPC codes applied; both also required a grace period (the forum quotes at least 10 minutes). Check the forum for the exact old wording before quoting it.
- **Sources:** Code cl. 5.2 and Annex B (`notices-and-codes.md`).

### CP2. Keying error
- **Point:** under the Code, a **minor** error (look-alike characters) must be cancelled (Annex F.1(h)). A **major** error (wrong or missing letters, swapped characters, the wrong car) must be reduced to £20 for 14 days (F.3(a)).
- **Applies when:** the tariff was paid and the registration was mistyped. **Late payment is a separate issue:** the Code's keying-error rules don't excuse paying after a deadline (e.g. an airport drop-off fee due by midnight the next day). Mention a late payment only as mitigation, and only in a way that doesn't identify the driver.
- **Evidence:** the payment record showing the registration entered.
- **Strength:** Strong for a minor error that wasn't cancelled. Weak for a major error where £20 was offered.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal:* ask for cancellation (minor), or for the £20 reduction (major) if the user would accept it.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* argue the operator failed to cancel, or failed to offer £20.
  - *Defence and WS:* the payment proves there was no loss; the breach was technical.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*
- **Sources:** Code Annex F; POPLA keying-error case studies.

### CP3. Other Code breaches
- **Point:** the operator broke the Code in issuing or pursuing the charge.
- **Applies when:**
  - Annex F.1 says the charge "must not be pursued" (e.g. emergencies; full payment before leaving an ANPR site; the operator breached a Code obligation about the notice);
  - the notice implies statutory authority or mimics a council PCN (cl. 8.1.1(a)–(b));
  - the notice claims POFA keeper liability where there's none (8.1.1(d));
  - the charge is over £100 (8.2.1);
  - no proper discount was offered (8.2.2);
  - the discount wasn't held after a rejected appeal (8.1.2(e)).
- **Strength:** Moderate at POPLA and IAS; Supporting only in court.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*
- **Sources:** Code text in `notices-and-codes.md`.

### CP4. A patrol officer watched but didn't intervene
- **Point:** where an on-foot attendant photographed the car (and perhaps the occupants walking away) but didn't speak to them, warn them or put a ticket on the car, the charge looks like a trap rather than parking management. Arguments: a party should take reasonable steps to avoid or reduce its loss (regulars: "Hiding behind a tree until you have left is unlikely to impress a judge", 5939613); a charge engineered by watching and waiting serves no legitimate interest of the kind *Beavis* relied on (C3, LS5); and it fits the "concealed pitfall or trap" line already in the template defence. Ask why no Notice to Driver was given, and put the operator to proof of the attendant's notes and full photo sequence.
- **Careful:** POFA allows a postal NTK under para 9 where no windscreen ticket was given, so present this as unfair conduct and a lack of legitimate interest, not as a broken rule. Also check the attendant met the Code's rules: ID (cl. 13.3) and photos meeting cl. 7.3.
- **Applies when:** the evidence shows an attendant was present (hand-held photos, photos of people) yet the first notice came by post. **Look at the photo timestamps:** a very short sequence (e.g. 20 seconds from first photo to the sign photo, 23 seconds to the charge time, in 6679995) shows the attendant didn't wait or check; Coupon-mad called that "predatory". Combine with C6: a charge issued within seconds gives no consideration period. (But note the Code's Annex B note: if an attendant observes the driver leave the location, the consideration period is treated as ended. That cuts against C6 in left-the-site cases.)
- **The attendant could have checked:** e.g. walked into the store to find the driver, or asked about a Blue Badge left inside the car (6679995). Ask the landowner or retailer why their contractor didn't.
- **Strength:** Supporting. *Confidence: Medium.*
- **By stage:** *Appeal:* one line. *POPLA and IAS:* ask for the attendant's notes and the full photo log. *Defence and WS:* brief, alongside LS1/C3.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 5939613; 6679995 (UKPC, Screwfix Chadwell Heath, Aug–Nov 2026: 20-second hand-held sequence, no windscreen ticket; Screwfix, a tenant, asked UKPC to cancel and was refused; the keeper later paid a £20 offer rather than go to POPLA).
- **To check:** in 6679995 Coupon-mad says using DVLA keeper data should be a "last resort" under ICO guidance. Not yet verified, so don't use it until the ICO source is found.

### CP5. Covert or undisclosed surveillance
- **Point:** operators must tell drivers that cameras are in use, as part of their data-protection obligations (the Code refers to signs "informing drivers of the use of camera surveillance equipment and associated information about their data privacy rights under the GDPR", cl. 3.1 Note 3). Camera vehicles "must not be used for covert surveillance" and must be clearly marked (cl. 7.2). Photo evidence must meet cl. 7.3 (clear registration, accurate timestamps, manual checks). If the charge rests on hidden cameras, an unmarked vehicle, or covert photography of people, put the operator to proof that its surveillance was disclosed and lawful.
- **Also:** a complaint to the ICO about covert or excessive surveillance is a separate route (the ICO looks at individual complaints, after a complaint to the company first; 6679354). It doesn't cancel the charge but adds pressure.
- **Applies when:** no camera signs; hidden or unmarked cameras or vehicles; photos of people taken without their knowledge; filming overnight or round the clock (6679354).
- **Strength:** Supporting at appeal and court; Moderate at POPLA/IAS where a clear Code breach is shown.
- **By stage:** *Appeal:* one line. *POPLA and IAS:* cite cl. 7.2/7.3 and the camera-sign requirement. *Defence:* put to proof. *Separately:* ICO complaint.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 6679354; 6436773 (patrol photos of people walking away at night).
- **Sources:** Single Code v1.1 cl. 3.1 Note 3, 7.2, 7.3, 13.3.

## M — Mitigation

### M1. Mitigating circumstances and honest mistakes
- **Point:** even if technically in breach, the circumstances make pursuing the charge unreasonable. The Code itself requires a reduction to £20, or cancellation.
- **Applies when:**
  - a permit or Blue Badge was held but not displayed (F.3(d)–(e) set £20 as the Code minimum; many operators cancel);
  - an expired permit was renewed promptly or backdated;
  - payment machines were broken with no alternative (F.3(f));
  - sudden illness, an overrunning medical appointment or a childcare delay (F.3(b));
  - a breakdown (F.3(c)).
- **Disability:** see the DS group (Equality Act reasonable adjustments, Blue Badge, accessibility signs).
- **Evidence:** a copy of the permit or badge, the renewal email, photos of the machine, medical letters, breakdown records.
- **Strength:** Moderate with good evidence. Strongest as a landowner complaint and at first appeal.
- **By stage:**
  - *Appeal and complaint:* lead with it.
  - *POPLA and IAS:* cite the Annex F item.
  - *Defence:* mention it briefly.
  - *WS:* expand with evidence; present it as an honest mistake, quickly corrected.
- **Never** describe an expired permit as "valid", or claim anything untrue.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*

## CT — Court-stage points

### CT1. The Particulars of Claim are defective
- **Point:**
  - Particulars that don't say what the breach was fail CPR 16.4 and can be struck out.
  - Factual errors in the Particulars (date, location, amount, vehicle) undermine the claim.
  - Particulars that rely on POFA, or on keeper liability, when the claimant's own documents say otherwise are contradictory (see L2).
- **Applies when:**
  - for the CPR 16.4 point: the breach isn't specified, or is as vague as "breach of the advertised terms and conditions". Judge this from the **Particulars**, not the NTK;
  - for errors: a factual mistake can be raised at any time. It's occasionally decisive (e.g. the user was elsewhere on the alleged date).
- **Defeated by:** a named breach. Leave the *Chan* paragraph out entirely.
- **Strength:** Strong where it applies.
- **Established wording:** the template defence's paragraph on *Chan*/*Akande* (see the template defence thread); *(for contradictions)* "Consequently, the Claimant's POC, which attempt to rely on Keeper liability 'and/or pursuant to POFA Sch 4', directly contradict their own prior admissions, documentary evidence, and statutory law. The pleading is defective and misleads the Court."
- **Forum threads:** 6473149 (*Chan* strike-out); 6637588.
- **Sources:** *CEL v Chan* (2023); *CPMS v Akande* (2024); CPR 16.4.

### CT2. No proper Letter of Claim (Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims)
- **Point:** the claimant issued without a compliant Letter of Claim, or didn't provide documents the debtor requested.
- **Applies when:** no Letter of Claim arrived, or a "fake" one only. Or the user asked for documents and the claim was issued within 30 days of them being provided (or without them).
- **Strength:** Supporting only. It mainly affects costs; ask the forum how to use it.
- **By stage:** *Defence:* state the facts. *WS:* expand.
- **Never** mention a genuine Letter of Claim that the user simply ignored.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*

### CT3. The add-on isn't recoverable
- **Point:** the parking charge already covers the operator's costs (*Beavis*). Adding a fixed fee (usually £60 or £70) is double recovery and an abuse of process.
- **Applies when:** almost every claim.
- **Strength:** Strong. Judges often disallow the add-on when it's challenged properly.
- **By stage:**
  - *LBC reply:* dispute it.
  - *Defence:* the template paragraph; ask for it to be struck out.
  - *WS:* cite *Excel v Wilkinson*.
- **Established wording:** *(defence)* "The Claimant has added an impermissible £[70] 'fee' to the £[100] parking charge. This artificial inflation is an unlawful attempt at double recovery. In *ParkingEye v Beavis* [2015], the Supreme Court held that a parking charge already covers all business operational and pre-action collection costs. Artificially inflating this claim to extract extra money constitutes an abuse of process."
- **If the add-on term is on the sign, check where and how big.** It's often in illegible small print at the bottom. A term the driver couldn't read was never incorporated into any contract (C8), and the CMA says add-on terms may be unenforceable if not incorporated, or if in small print or vague (paras 37, 41). Ask for a close-up photo of it.
- **CMA support (July 2026):** the CMA's open letter to operators says the Industry Code's allowance of up to £70 "does not itself provide a legal basis" to make consumers liable for the fee (para 38). Such terms may be unenforceable if not properly incorporated, unfair under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 s.62, or not transparent under s.68 (para 37). Its concerns include vague wording ("may"), unquantified sums, small print, and fees charged by third-party debt collectors (para 41). Cite it alongside *Wilkinson*; it's the regulator's view, not a court ruling.
- **Forum threads:** 6279348 (*Wilkinson* and exhibits); 6679952 (CEL defence citing the CMA letter).
- **Sources:** *Excel v Wilkinson* (2020); *Beavis*; CMA open letter, 16 July 2026, paras 35–43.

### CT4. Keeper liability is capped at the original charge
- **Point:** if POFA keeper liability did apply, para 4(5) limits it to the amount specified in the NTK. Add-ons aren't recoverable from the keeper.
- **Applies when:** as a fallback argument ("if POFA applied, which is denied").
- **Strength:** Moderate as a fallback.
- **Established wording:** *(defence)* "In the alternative, if POFA Schedule 4 did apply (which is denied), Paragraph 4(5) caps registered keeper liability at the original parking charge amount (£[100]). Additional fees are statutorily barred under POFA."
- **Forum threads:** 6637588.
- **Sources:** POFA Sch 4 para 4(5).

### CT5. Interest is discretionary
- **Point:** interest under s.69 County Courts Act 1984 is at the court's discretion. Ask the court to refuse it where the claim is inflated or contradicts the claimant's own admissions.
- **Strength:** Supporting only.
- **Established wording:** *(defence)* "Section 69 interest under the County Courts Act 1984 is a discretionary remedy. Given that the underlying claim entirely lacks merit [and contradicts the Claimant's own written admissions], the Court is urged to exercise its discretion to deny all interest."
- **Forum threads:** 6637588.

### CT6. Unfair terms (Consumer Rights Act 2015)
- **Point:** the court must consider fairness (s.71). Terms that create a significant imbalance (Sch 2), or that aren't transparent and prominent (s.68), aren't binding on a consumer.
- **Applies when:** the charge is disproportionate to the event, or the terms are unreadable from a moving vehicle or hidden behind links.
- **Strength:** Supporting only.
- **Established wording:** *(none yet)*
- **Forum threads:** 6637588.
- **Sources:** Consumer Rights Act 2015 ss.62, 68, 71, Sch 2.

## W — Weak alone, or special purpose

### W1. "I never received the NTK"
- **Point:** put the claimant to proof of posting and delivery.
- **Weakness:** POFA presumes delivery unless the contrary is proved (9(6)), so this rarely wins alone.
- **Honesty:** if the NTK arrived and was thrown away, say "I do not hold a copy" (or "has no record of receiving" if true), never "it was never sent". Ask for copies (SAR / Pre-Action Protocol).
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*

### W2. Default judgment entered at an old address (set-aside)
- **Point:** where a claim went to an out-of-date DVLA address, a default judgment can be set aside. The claimant must take reasonable steps if it has reason to believe the address is out of date.
- **Strength:** Strong where the facts fit. Act promptly.
- **Forum threads:** *(none yet)*
- **Sources:** *Carr v VCS* [2025] EWCA Civ 713; `hearing-and-ccj.md`.
